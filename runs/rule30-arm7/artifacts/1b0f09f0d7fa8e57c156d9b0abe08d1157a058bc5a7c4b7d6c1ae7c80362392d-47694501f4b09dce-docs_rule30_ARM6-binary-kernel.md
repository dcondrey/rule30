# Arm 6: finite binary-query elimination

Probe: `experiments/rule30/automaticity_probe.py`.

This arm follows the failed Crosstalk elimination run recorded on 2026-08-27.
The run's evolved candidates all proposed a direct parity query or dyadic
divide-and-conquer over the binary digits of `n`, but none supplied the required
composition law. Most fell directly into ARM5's already-falsified dyadic block
cancellation family. The one residual interpretation that is precise enough to
test cheaply is a fixed finite-state binary-digit query.

## 1. Exact target class

For center sequence `a(n)`, its 2-kernel is

```text
K_2(a) = { (a(2^e k + r))_{k>=0} : e>=0, 0<=r<2^e }.
```

A finite 2-kernel is equivalent to 2-automaticity and supplies a fixed-state
way to query `a(n)` from the binary digits of `n`. A finite-dimensional linear
span of this kernel over GF(2) is the corresponding bounded-rank transfer model.
These are precise subclasses of the proposed direct-query route. They are not
all possible divide-and-conquer algorithms.

## 2. Pre-registered kill conditions

Before running the probe:

1. If all `2^12 = 4096` residuals at depth 12 have distinct 64-bit prefixes, an
   exact finite-kernel representation needs at least 4096 residual states. This
   kills a *small* fixed-state explanation, not finite automaticity itself.
2. If the depth-9 residual-prefix matrix reaches GF(2) rank 512, a linear
   representation needs dimension at least 512. This kills a small bounded-rank
   transfer operator, not one whose dimension grows with `n`.

Thue--Morse is the positive control. Its 2-kernel should saturate at two
residuals with GF(2) rank two.

## 3. Verification

The Rule 30 prefix is generated by the repository's bit-parallel ground truth.
The probe checks its first 32 bits against the pinned known prefix and its first
256 bits against a separate cell-array implementation before measuring the
kernel. The control and target use identical measurement code.

Command:

```bash
python3 experiments/rule30/automaticity_probe.py \
  --identity-prefix 128 --rank-prefix 1024
```

The run generates `524288` center bits. Results:

| depth | residuals | distinct 128-bit prefixes | cumulative distinct | GF(2) rank |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 4 | 16 | 16 | 31 | 16 |
| 6 | 64 | 64 | 127 | 64 |
| 8 | 256 | 256 | 511 | 256 |
| 9 | 512 | 512 | 1023 | 512 |
| 10 | 1024 | 1024 | 2047 | 512 |
| 11 | 2048 | 2048 | 4095 | 256 |
| 12 | 4096 | 4096 | 8191 | 128 |

The rank falls above depth 9 only because the fixed total prefix leaves fewer
sample columns per residual (`524288 / 2^e`); it is an observation-window cap,
not measured algebraic collapse. At depth 9 the 512 residuals have 1024 samples
each and attain the maximum possible row rank of 512.

Thue--Morse returns exactly two cumulative residuals and rank two at every depth
from 1 through 12.

## 4. Result and boundary

Both kill conditions fire. Rule 30 exhibits the maximal possible number of
distinct sampled residuals at every measured depth, while the linear rank grows
to the full 512 allowed by the depth-9 row count. Therefore:

- any exact 2-kernel for the center sequence has at least 8191 distinct residual
  sequences (the observed prefixes distinguish them rigorously);
- any exact GF(2)-linear representation of those sampled residuals needs
  dimension at least 512;
- the small fixed-state and small bounded-rank versions of the direct binary
  query are closed.

Finite data cannot establish that the 2-kernel or its rank is unbounded. This
arm is not a proof of non-automaticity and says nothing about algorithms outside
the fixed-kernel/linear-representation classes. A proof route would need a
family of residuals that can be shown distinct for arbitrarily large depth.
