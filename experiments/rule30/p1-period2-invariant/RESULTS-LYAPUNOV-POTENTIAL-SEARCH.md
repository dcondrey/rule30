# Lyapunov/potential search on the growing queue: orbits are too short to test a density, and every count/entropy/distance candidate tried is falsified anyway

Date: 2026-09-06

Status: **EVERY TESTED CANDIDATE (SIX RAW COUNTS, FIVE LENGTH-NORMALIZED
DENSITIES, ONE BOUNDED CAYLEY DISTANCE) IS FALSIFIED WITHIN A FEW STEPS FROM
A SHORT START, BUT THE DENSITY FALSIFICATIONS ARE THE SAME NUMERATOR
ARTIFACT AS THE RAW COUNTS, NOT INDEPENDENT DYNAMICAL EVIDENCE: QUEUE
LIFETIMES ON THIS OBJECT (2-11 STEPS, PER `EXPECTED_MAXIMA`) ARE TOO SHORT
FOR A LENGTH-NORMALIZED STATISTIC TO BE TESTABLE AT ALL. THE ONE GENUINE
NEGATIVE RESULT IS THE BOUNDED-ABOVE RECURRENCE CANDIDATE IN SECTION 4. THIS
IS A NEGATIVE RESULT ON THE SEARCHED CANDIDATES, NOT A PROOF THAT NO
POTENTIAL EXISTS.**

Evidence level: `K` (exact counterexamples within a bounded search).

## 1. What this does and does not test

`RESULTS-QUEUE-WINDOW-PHASE-NO-GO.md` proves that no fixed-radius end
window, augmented by queue length, endpoint/event data, and the exact total
`D8` action, can be a transition CONGRUENCE (a finite state compression
compatible with transitions) for the normalized queue, at any finite radius.
Its proof (section 4) is specifically about finite quotients: the interior
blocks `00` and `11` collapse to the same `D8` identity element but are
dynamically distinguishable one step later.

That theorem has nothing to say about a real- or integer-valued function
`Phi` on the full queue that (a) need not factor through any finite
quotient and (b) only has to be monotone (or bounded, forcing a
contradiction) along an actual orbit, not transition-compatible in the
congruence sense. This is a different proof technology (an
analytic/counting descent argument, as used successfully for the
zero-prefix greedy bound in `RESULTS-SCALE-TELESCOPING.md`) rather than an
automaton compression. The present work searches that different space, over
the exact object carrying `(SEP)` and the arbitrary-queue-mortality target
of `PROOF-STATE-CAPSULE.md`: the growing normalized queue of
`constant_tail_queue.py`.

It is a genuinely different target from `RESULTS-SCALE-TELESCOPING.md`: that
result counts forced-continuation tokens on hard-core endpoint words `W`
under a fixed cut tail (the `s_c(W) <= n` / `n+1` conjecture); this work
tests functions of the queue object itself. No counting-token bound from
that document is reused or duplicated here.

It is also worth noting explicitly: `PROOF-STATE-CAPSULE.md` section 5
already records one prior potential-style attempt, "additive
position-indexed weight on the anti-diagonal (subinvariant/Lyapunov
certificate)" (`RESULTS-SUBINVARIANT-CERTIFICATE.md`), which found an exact
Farkas infeasibility for every *linear, position-indexed* weighting on the
RW survivor edges. That is a different object (the anti-diagonal / RW
survivor-edge graph, restricted to linear weightings) from the one tested
here (the full queue vector, with nonlinear candidates such as entropy and
Cayley distance). The present search does not repeat that LP search.

**Candidate selection was not pre-registered before any exploration.** An
informal exploratory pass (uninstrumented, not committed) was run first over
a handful of candidate shapes to see which were even worth writing down; the
six raw-count candidates and the Cayley-distance candidate below are the
ones that survived that filter enough to be worth stating as formal claims.
This section reports those formal claims and their kill conditions
honestly, but the claims below are *post-hoc registrations of a bounded
search*, not predictions made and then tested blind. The five density
candidates and the recurrence-bound candidate were added after that first
round of falsifications, specifically to close the gap flagged in review
(see section 3).

## 2. The object under test

Orbits are generated by the exact, unmodified queue cocycle in
`constant_tail_queue.py`: `queue_step` applies one constant-cut-tail update
(a right-permutive scan that rewrites every existing queue entry to its
scan state and appends one new boundary symbol), and `normalize_queue`
collapses the scan-indistinguishable symbols `1` and `3`. **The queue grows
by exactly one symbol on every successful step.** An orbit dies (returns
`None`) when the trailing symbol from the previous step is not in `{1, 2}`.
This is the same machinery already used by the no-go theorem
(`constant_tail_queue_window_phase_no_go.py`) and by
`arbitrary_queue_census`, whose exhaustive finite lifetimes
(`EXPECTED_MAXIMA` in `constant_tail_queue.py`) are the numerical evidence
motivating the (open) conjecture that every queue eventually dies.

The search enumerates every normalized-representative legal starting queue
of length 1 through 11 (ternary middle alphabet, as in
`arbitrary_queue_census`, since every four-symbol middle word shares its
`3->1`-normalized future) for both tails 2 and 3, runs each to its actual
death (`lifetime`), and tests every candidate along every resulting orbit.
18,770 orbits were tested at `--max-length 11 --orbit-cap 15`.

## 3. Why the first six candidates are a weaker test than they look

Because the queue length strictly increases every step, any **extensive**
statistic (a raw count summed over the whole queue) cannot be non-increasing
in general on structural grounds alone: appending one symbol can only add to
most such counts. A falsification of `zero_count` (it goes `0 -> 0 -> 1`
because a `0` gets appended) or `adjacent_equal_count` is consistent with
this arithmetic fact and need not say anything about the dynamics. Six of
the twelve candidates below are of this extensive form; their
falsifications are reported for completeness (they were the ones actually
run first) but are not treated as informative about the neutral-block
mechanism.

The `action_cayley_distance` candidate has the opposite problem: the queue
input-symbol action group is exactly `D8` (confirmed: 8 elements, diameter 2
under the three generators used here), so a candidate valued in `{0,1,2}`
cannot serve as a descent certificate over an orbit of more than 2 steps
regardless of what the dynamics do. It is retained as a negative control on
the specific object named by the no-go theorem's equation (7), not as a
serious candidate.

The five **density** candidates (sections 3.2) and the recurrence-gap
candidate (section 4) are the real tests: they normalize by the queue
length (or bound a state-recurrence gap), so their falsification is not an
arithmetic artifact of growth and does actually reflect the queue
dynamics.

### 3.1 Extensive candidates (weak test; falsified, arithmetic in part)

Claim tested: **non-increasing along every step of every legal orbit.**
Kill condition: one step where the value strictly increases.

| Candidate | Definition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `adjacent_equal_count` | count of adjacent equal symbol pairs in the normalized queue | **FALSIFIED**, tail=2 start=`(2,1,1)` step=1, `0 -> 1` |
| `weighted_equal_count` | adjacent equal pairs weighted by distance from the scanned end, `sum(n-i)` | **FALSIFIED**, tail=2 start=`(2,1,1)` step=1, `0 -> 4` |
| `negative_run_count` | negative of the number of maximal constant runs | **FALSIFIED**, tail=2 start=`(2,0,1,2)` step=1, `-5 -> -4` |
| `symbol_entropy` | Shannon entropy (bits) of the symbol-frequency distribution (already intensive; kept here since it was tested alongside the others) | **FALSIFIED**, tail=2 start=`(2,1,1)` step=0, `0.918 -> 1.000` |
| `zero_count` | count of symbol `0` | **FALSIFIED**, tail=2 start=`(2,1,1)` step=1, `0 -> 1` |
| `negative_two_count` | negative of the count of symbol `2` | **FALSIFIED**, tail=2 start=`(2,0,2,2)` step=0, `-3 -> -2` |
| `action_cayley_distance` | `D8` Cayley-graph distance from the identity of the exact total word action of the nonleading queue (bounded 0-2; see caveat above) | **FALSIFIED**, tail=2 start=`(2,1,1)` step=0, `0 -> 2` |

The shortest witness orbit, tail 2, start `(2, 1, 1)`:

```text
(2, 1, 1)  -->  (2, 1, 2, 1)  -->  (2, 1, 1, 0, 2)   [dies next step]
```

kills five of the seven candidates above. `negative_run_count` needs
`(2, 0, 1, 2)` (raw run count falls from 5 to 4, so its negation rises from
`-5` to `-4`); `negative_two_count` needs `(2, 0, 2, 2)`.

### 3.2 Density candidates (falsified, but not testably so)

Claim tested: **non-increasing along every step of every legal orbit,**
same kill condition, applied to the length-normalized versions of five of
the above.

| Candidate | Definition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `adjacent_equal_density` | `adjacent_equal_count / n` | **FALSIFIED**, tail=2 start=`(2,1,1)` step=1, `0.0 -> 0.2` |
| `weighted_equal_density` | `weighted_equal_count / n^2` | **FALSIFIED**, tail=2 start=`(2,1,1)` step=1, `0.0 -> 0.16` |
| `negative_run_density` | `-run_count / n` | **FALSIFIED**, tail=2 start=`(2,1,1)` step=1, `-1.0 -> -0.8` |
| `zero_density` | `zero_count / n` | **FALSIFIED**, tail=2 start=`(2,1,1)` step=1, `0.0 -> 0.2` |
| `negative_two_density` | `-two_count / n` | **FALSIFIED**, tail=2 start=`(2,1,1)` step=1, `-0.5 -> -0.4` |

Dividing by length only closes the escape hatch for a count that
*accumulates* over an ever-longer queue. It does not close two other
arithmetic shapes, and inspection of the actual numerators on the witness
orbit `(2,1,1) -> (2,1,2,1) -> (2,1,1,0,2)` (lengths 3, 4, 5) shows both are
present here, not the accumulation case:

```text
negative_run_density:  raw run count 2, 4, 4 (frozen at the kill step);
                        -4/4 = -1.0  ->  -4/5 = -0.8   (denominator only)
negative_two_density:  raw two count 1, 2, 2 (frozen at the kill step);
                        -2/4 = -0.5  ->  -2/5 = -0.4   (denominator only)
zero_density, adjacent_equal_density, weighted_equal_density:
                        raw count 0 -> 0 -> 1 (first appearance of the
                        counted feature); dividing by n does nothing to a
                        0 -> 1 step.
```

So every one of the five density falsifications is, on this witness orbit,
the same underlying event as the corresponding raw-count falsification in
section 3.1 (a frozen numerator against a growing denominator, or a count's
first appearance), not an independent dynamical fact. "The densities jump
upward at the same step as the raw counts" is evidence they share a cause,
not evidence the cause is dynamical.

The root problem is scale: `EXPECTED_MAXIMA` in `constant_tail_queue.py`
shows queue lifetimes in the searched range (2-15) capping out around 9-11
steps, and the witness orbits found here die within 2-4 steps. Over an
orbit that short, a length-normalized statistic is dominated by a single
`±1` change in numerator or denominator; there is no regime in which a real
descent trend, as opposed to a one-step accumulation artifact, could show
up. **Density candidates are not testable on this queue cocycle at these
orbit lengths**, independent of which specific density is chosen.

All twelve monotonicity candidates and their exact witnesses are asserted
as a regression check inside `lyapunov_potential_search.py`
(`registered_witnesses`), independent of the live search parameters; the
regression check is a reproducibility record, not a claim that all twelve
falsifications are equally informative (only the six of section 3.1, read
as arithmetic controls, and the bounded-above candidate of section 4, are).

## 4. Bounded-above candidate: recurrence to a reference scan state

The task also named a second shape: a quantity bounded above in a way that
forces a contradiction, rather than strictly decreasing. Since orbit death
here means "eventually the trailing symbol is not `1` or `2`", a natural
bounded-above candidate is the recurrence gap of `final_scan_state` to a
fixed reference value.

**Pre-registration.** Let `g(t)` = number of steps since `final_scan_state`
last equaled `0` (or since orbit start). Motivated by a small hand-sampled
set of orbits in which `final_scan_state` alternated mostly between `0` and
`2`, the claim tested was `g(t) <= 2` for every step of every legal orbit.
Kill condition: one orbit step with `g(t) > 2`.

**Result: FALSIFIED.** The full search's first violation is tail=2,
start=`(2, 0, 1, 1, 0, 1, 1)`, step 2, gap 3 (states `(2, 2, 2)` at that
point). A separately checked longer orbit, tail=2, start
`(2, 0, 0, 1, 2, 1, 0, 2, 0, 1, 2)`, reaches gap 5 later in the same
trajectory (states `(2,2,2,2,2,0,2,0)`, gaps `(1,2,3,4,5,0,1,0)`). So the
naive fixed-bound recurrence claim fails outright; `final_scan_state` can
stay away from `0` for a growing, not merely bounded, number of steps
within the tested range. No boundedness conclusion is drawn beyond "not
bounded by 2, and not obviously bounded by any small constant in this
search."

## 5. Neutral-block probe on the no-go theorem's own counterexample family

To check the claim (made only informally in an earlier draft of this
document) that the same neutral-block mechanism defeating the finite
quotients also defeats these densities, `adjacent_equal_density` was
evaluated directly on `RESULTS-QUEUE-WINDOW-PHASE-NO-GO.md`'s own
`R^0_k`/`R^1_k` (tail 2) and `S^0_k`/`S^1_k` (tail 3) pairs, at radii
1, 3, 5, 9, 15:

```text
tail=2 k=1:  00-block=0.1667  11-block=0.3333  (differ)
tail=2 k=3:  00-block=0.5000  11-block=0.6000  (differ)
tail=2 k=5:  00-block=0.6429  11-block=0.7143  (differ)
tail=2 k=9:  00-block=0.7727  11-block=0.8182  (differ)
tail=2 k=15: 00-block=0.8529  11-block=0.8824  (differ)

tail=3 k=2:  00-block=0.3750  11-block=0.3750  (equal)
tail=3 k=6:  00-block=0.3500  11-block=0.3500  (equal)
tail=3 k=10: 00-block=0.3438  11-block=0.3438  (equal)
tail=3 k=18: 00-block=0.3393  11-block=0.3393  (equal)
```

For tail 2 the density genuinely distinguishes the `00`-block queue from the
`11`-block queue: the `11` block extends the preceding run of `1`s by one
adjacent-equal pair that the `00` block, sitting between a `1` and a `2`,
does not, so the two densities differ by exactly one pair out of a queue of
length `n = 2k + 4`. The gap is therefore exactly `1/(2k+4)`:

```text
k= 1 (n= 6): gap = 1/6  = 0.1667
k= 3 (n=10): gap = 1/10 = 0.1000
k= 5 (n=14): gap = 1/14 = 0.0714
k= 9 (n=22): gap = 1/22 = 0.0455
k=15 (n=34): gap = 1/34 = 0.0294
```

All five match the measured values to four decimals. **The gap decays like
`Theta(1/k)` and is not bounded away from zero**: it is exactly one fixed
absolute difference (a single adjacent-equal pair) diluted by a growing
length, the same one-`+1`-against-a-growing-denominator effect documented
in section 3.2. It does not survive as `k -> infinity`. For tail 3 the two
densities are exactly equal at every tested radius, because the block sits
inside an alternating `(01)^k` context where neither `00` nor `11` creates
an extra same-symbol neighbor. So `adjacent_equal_density` sees the
neutral-block distinction for tail 2 only as a vanishing perturbation, and
does not see it at all for tail 3, in this specific family; this is
reported as data, not as an explanation, since these particular `R`/`S`
families are not shown (here or in the no-go doc) to correspond to
genuinely mortal or immortal orbits on their own.

## 6. Interpretation

Twelve monotonicity candidates and one bounded-above recurrence claim were
tested; all thirteen were falsified within a few steps from short legal
starts, but not all falsifications carry equal weight:

- The six extensive counts (section 3.1) and the five densities
  (section 3.2) fail for the same underlying reason: legal queue orbits on
  this object die within 2-11 steps (`EXPECTED_MAXIMA`), and over an orbit
  that short a length-normalized statistic cannot be distinguished from a
  raw count's one-step arithmetic (a first-appearing symbol, or a frozen
  numerator against a growing denominator). This is a genuine constraint on
  the proof search, not a dynamical finding: **counts and densities of the
  current queue content are not testable candidates at these orbit
  lengths**, and a future attempt in this direction needs either much
  longer-lived orbits or a statistic immune to the ±1-against-growth
  problem.
- `action_cayley_distance` fails for an unrelated structural reason: the
  underlying group is `D8` (diameter 2), so no function valued there can
  serve as a descent certificate past two steps regardless of dynamics.
- The recurrence-to-a-reference-state candidate (section 4) is the one
  genuine dynamical negative result here: it does not involve length
  normalization or a growing-denominator artifact, and it is falsified by
  an actual multi-step drift of `final_scan_state` away from the reference
  value (gap growing from 1 to 5 within one orbit), which is real evidence
  that a fixed-bound recurrence claim on the scan state does not hold.

This does not establish that no potential exists. It does establish that
naive counts/densities of current queue content are the wrong shape to test
on this object at all (orbits are too short), and that a naive bounded
recurrence on the scan state is false. A workable candidate would need
either much longer test orbits (to make a density statistic meaningful) or
a quantity that uses the *position* of a block relative to future scan
events in a way that survives the rewrite (section 5 shows a
position-dependent gap exists in principle for tail 2, but it vanishes as
`Theta(1/k)`, so it is not that quantity either); this is the same
direction `PROOF-STATE-CAPSULE.md` section 6, cross-analysis item 4,
already flags as unexplored: a phase-decorated ordered token count, not a
scalar count of the current state.

```bash
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 uv run --project experiments/sygus-p3 python \
  experiments/rule30/p1-period2-invariant/lyapunov_potential_search.py \
  --max-length 11 --orbit-cap 15
```

Prints, for each of the twelve monotonicity candidates, either the exact
falsifying witness (orbit start, step, before/after value, and the two
queues involved) or `NO VIOLATION FOUND`; reports the recurrence-bound
witness; prints the neutral-block density probe on the no-go doc's `R`/`S`
families; asserts the hardcoded regression witnesses reproduce exactly;
exits 0.
