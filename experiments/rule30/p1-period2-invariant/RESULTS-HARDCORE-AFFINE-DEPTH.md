# The pinned left half-plane is affine in the hard-core endpoint word only to column −7

Date: 2026-09-16. Script `hardcore-affinity/hardcore_affine.py`, log
`hardcore_affine_M22.log`. No pre-registration; the expected outcome stated
before the run was "affine to an unbounded or linearly growing depth", and it
was wrong.

**`[C]` With the centre pinned to `0101...`, write `rho_k = s(2k, 1)` for the
even-time column-1 word. The left half-plane is a function of `rho` alone
(`s(t,-1) = 1` at odd `t`, `1 - rho_(t/2)` at even `t`, then
`s(t,-j-1) = s(t+1,-j) XOR (s(t,-j) OR s(t,-j+1))`), and the right half-plane
forces `rho` to be hard-core (`rho_k rho_(k+1) = 0`). On the hard-core domain
every cell of columns `-1 .. -7` at even `t` (`-1 .. -6` at odd `t`) is an
affine form over `F_2` in the bits of `rho`; from column `-8` (`-7` at odd
`t`) a cell needs products of non-adjacent bits, and from column `-14`
(`-15` at odd `t`) it needs degree at least 3 (all 46,368 hard-core words of length 22,
cells `t <= 3`, `j <= 35`). The nonlinearity that the hard-core condition
removes at the boundary re-enters seven columns in and its degree then grows
with depth, so the finiteness constraints on the left seed are not a linear
system over `F_2`, and the linear route this would have opened is closed.**

## 1. Why it was worth asking

The OR in Rule 30 generates products of adjacent cells. Near the pinned
centre the adjacent pairs are `(rho_k, rho_(k+1))` in disguise: by hand,
`s(2k,-2) = rho_k`, `s(2k,-3) = 1 + rho_(k+1)`, `s(2k,-4) = rho_k rho_(k+1)
= 0`, `s(2k,-5) = 1 + rho_(k+1) + rho_(k+2)`, `s(2k,-6) = rho_(k+2)`, every
product killed by the hard-core identity. Had that continued, the row-0
finiteness constraints `s(0,-j) = 0`, `j > a`, would have been an `F_2`-linear
system intersected with the golden-mean shift, whose solutions have the
dyadic (power-of-two period) structure the archive keeps meeting, and
period-two exclusion would have become a rank question.

## 2. Result

Degree of the cell `(t, -j)` on the hard-core domain, `j = 1..35` left to
right (`1` affine, `2` needs non-adjacent products, `3` means at least 3):

```text
t=0: 11111112222223333333333333333333333
t=1: 11111121222222323333333333333333333
t=2: 11111112222223333333333333333333333
t=3: 11111121222222323333333333333333333
```

First non-affine column: `-8` at even `t`, `-7` at odd `t`; first column of
degree at least 3: `-14` at even `t` and `-15` at odd `t` (at odd `t` column
`-16` is of degree 2 again and degree 3 resumes from `-17`). Position `j` in
the rows above is column `-j`; the "`-15` (`-16`)" that stood here until
2026-09-17 misread them by one. The products that appear at column `-8` are of bits at distance 2
and more, which no factor of the right language kills.

## 3. Reading

The hard-core condition linearizes exactly one light-cone width of the left
half-plane and no more. Beyond it the degree climbs at roughly one per seven
columns on this range, in agreement with the polynomial-calculus finding
that refutations over seed bits need full degree
(`RESULTS-PT2-PC-DEGREE.md`). This closes the idea in its clean form. What
remains of it is descriptive: the first nonlinear layer at column `-8` is
generated by a small, explicit set of non-adjacent products, and the right
language's longer forbidden factors constrain those bit pairs; nobody has
written down which products survive both sides. `RW`, `SEP`, `PT2` untouched.
