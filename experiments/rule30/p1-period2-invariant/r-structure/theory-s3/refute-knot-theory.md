## The attempt's CONJ is a lemma: the section count is unbounded and `c` is not finite-state (U)

Added in a follow-up pass the same day. Checks: `scripts/refute_knot-theory_unbounded.py`, log `_unbounded.log`, exit 0.

**Lemma U-a (sections as maps).** For any prefixes `u, v`, of any lengths, `g|_u = g|_v` iff `Phi_u = Phi_v`, where `Phi_u(y) = F^(|u|)(uy)`. *Proof.* `g|_u(y)_t = c_(|u|+t)(uy) = F^t(F^(|u|)(uy))_1 = c(Phi_u(y))_t`, so `g|_u = c o Phi_u`, and `c` is injective (item 1). This extends item 4 across depths.

**Lemma U-b (outward edge).** If `x` is finite and nonzero with its last 1 at position `p`, then `F^t(x)` has its last 1 at exactly `p + t`. *Proof.* Induction on `t`, with hypothesis "cell `p+t` is 1 and every cell beyond it is 0" (so the row stays finite). The cell `p+t+1` at time `t+1` reads `(x_(p+t+2), x_(p+t+1), x_(p+t)) = (0, 0, 1)`, and `0 XOR (0 OR 1) = 1`. Every cell `m >= p+t+2` reads cells `m-1..m+1`, all beyond `p+t`, hence three zeros, so it stays 0. That re-establishes the hypothesis at `t+1`. The wall enters only through the left neighbour `x_0` of cell 1, and `p+t+1 >= 2`. [C: `_unbounded.log` U1, every nonzero `u` of length `<= 14`, `t <= 20`.]

**Theorem.** `count(n) >= n + 1` for every `n`, so the section count is unbounded, and `c` has infinitely many distinct sections, so it is not finite-state. *Proof.* At depth `n`, take `u_0 = 0^n` and `u_p = 0^(p-1) 1 0^(n-p)` for `p = 1..n`. `Phi_(u_0)(0^inf) = 0`, and by U-b `Phi_(u_p)(0^inf)` has its last 1 at `p + n`. These `n + 1` values are pairwise distinct, so by U-a the sections are too. [C: `_unbounded.log` U2, the `n + 1` classes are distinct under the exact criterion for `n = 1..10`.]

Consequences. Bridge 3's automaton-group arm is closed as U, not C: no finite automaton computes `c`, so the Delacourt–Ollinger correspondence cannot extend to Rule 30 through the wall trace. The attempt's "Proving it needs an explicit family of pairwise distinct sections" is answered by the family `u_p`. The attempt also treated "unbounded per depth" and "not finite-state" as the same statement. Only the first implies the second, but both now hold. The alternative route (a finite seed with a provably non-eventually-periodic wall trace, since a Mealy automaton maps eventually periodic inputs to eventually periodic outputs) is no longer needed.

The theorem is linear; the measured growth is exponential. [C: `_unbounded.log` U3] The distinguisher `y = 0^inf` alone already separates `1, 2, 4, 6, 11, 17, 28, 47, 80, 130, 205, 343, 571` classes at `n = 0..12`: the number of distinct `F^n(u 0^inf)`, `u in {0,1}^n`, which is a lower bound on `count(n)` by U-a. Its ratios are also near `5/3` (`571/343 = 1.665`). So an exponential lower bound would follow from an exponential lower bound on `|F^n({0,1}^n 0^inf)|`, an image-counting question on finite rows in the territory of `attempt-backward-tree.md` Lemma 2. CONJ.

## Why the exponential bound resists the U-b method (negative, C)

Follow-up pass, same day. Target: `count(n) >= mu^n` for some `mu > 1`. Not reached; the growth stays CONJ. Script `scripts/refute_knot-theory_growth.py`, log `_growth.log`, exit 0. Witnesses are written `x_1` first, so the wall is on the left.

- **Same-section pairs differ right up to the outer end** (G1). For every `n = 3..11`, the largest outermost differing bit among prefix pairs with equal sections is exactly `n - 1`. Witnesses: `0^(n-3)101 ~ 0^(n-3)011` at `n = 3, 5, 7, 9, 11`, and `000101 ~ 100011`, `0000000101 ~ 0100100011` at `n = 6, 10`. So no lemma of the form "equal sections force agreement on the outer `alpha n` bits" exists. Every extension of U-b that separates prefixes by where their outer bits sit hits this. Largest class sizes are `1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 14, 24, 35, 56` at `n = 2..11`. A proof that the largest class is at most `nu^n` with `nu < 2` would give `count(n) >= (2/nu)^n`. The measured ratio near 1.5 per step leaves room for that, but no such bound is proved.
- **Sparse prefix families are not section-injective** (G2). For ones spaced at least `g` apart, the first collision comes at `n = 4, 8, 8` for `g = 2, 3, 4`. Every witness toggles only wall-adjacent bits (`0010 ~ 1010`; `00000010 ~ 10000010`), because the wall is where `F` loses a bit.
- **Image counting cannot give the bound** (G3). `|F^k({0,1}^n 0^inf)|` keeps shrinking in `k`: at `n = 20` the step ratios climb from 0.523 to 0.955 with no floor. `I(n) = |F^n({0,1}^n 0^inf)|` is a valid lower bound on `count(n)` by U-a, but it is the smaller object; bounding it below is at least as hard as the target.
- **Backward-tree tails are weaker still** (G4). The distinct tails beyond `n` of the `2^n` points of `F^(-n)(0^inf)` give another lower bound by U-a, since `Phi_u^(-1)(0)` separates. They number `1305` at `n = 14`, against `I(14) = 1548`.
- **No linear-algebra separation** (G5). In the GF(2) algebraic normal form of `K_(n+t)(u; y)` in `y`, the coefficient functions that are affine in `u` have linear parts of rank `2, 2, 1, 0, 0, 0` at `n = 2..7`. From `n = 5` on they are all constants.
- **Full-speed front control yields only O(n)** (sketch, not U). A difference whose outermost position is `q` survives to time `n` if the free outward diagonal starting at cell `q+1` is zero while its inputs lie inside `u`. After that, the fresh `y` bits (permutive at the outer light-cone edge) keep the front advancing. But a zero outward diagonal at `k` needs `x_k = 0` and diagonals `k+1`, `k+2` equal. Two zero diagonals at `k` and `k+2` therefore force every diagonal beyond `k` to be zero. So good branch positions occur only next to the zero region, which is U-b again.

What an exponential bound needs, as far as this pass can tell, is control of the damage front on Rule 30's non-permutive side at a positive but sub-unit speed, adversarially over `u`. That is a Lyapunov-exponent statement for the OR direction. Its value near 0.24 is quoted from memory of the numerical literature, not re-checked here, and it is not proved here. A heuristic, not a computation: if pairs whose outermost difference `q` exceeds about `0.38 n` separate and the rest collide, a collision-count (Cauchy–Schwarz) bound predicts `mu` near `2^0.62 = 1.54`, the same order as the measured `5/3`.

## Triangle theorem: sections are decided on two diagonals (U)

Fourth follow-up pass, same day. Script `scripts/refute_knot-theory_triangle.py`, log `_triangle_N22.log`, exit 0. It supersedes the lookahead and window measurements above as a way to decide section equality.

**Setup.** Fix prefixes `u, v` of length `n` and run both zero-wall diagrams from `uy` and `vy`. Cell `(t, j)` reads `x_(j-t)..x_(j+t)`, so the triangle `T = {(t, j) : j >= 1, j + t <= n}` depends on the prefix alone, not on `y`. Write `B_t = F^t(u)_(n-t)` for `t = 0..n-1` (the hypotenuse) and `C_t = F^(t-1)(u)_(n-t)` for `t = 1..n` (one diagonal in; `C_n` is the wall cell, 0). Call a cell damaged at time `t` if the two diagrams differ there.

**Theorem.** `g|_u = g|_v` iff `B_t(u) = B_t(v)` for all `t`, and `C_t(u) = C_t(v)` for every `t` with `B_(t-1) = 0`. Consequently `count(n)` equals the number of distinct signatures `(B, C restricted to B_(t-1) = 0)` over `u in {0,1}^n`, computable in `O(n^2)` per prefix.

*Proof.* Cells outside `T` at time `t` are those with `j >= n - t + 1`. An outside cell reads only outside cells, except in three places. (i) Cell `n-t+1` reads `n-t` and `n-t+1` through the OR, and its permutive input `n-t+2` is outside. (ii) Cell `n-t+2` reads the hypotenuse cell `n-t+1` through the OR. (iii) At `t = n`, cell 1 reads cell 1 permutively (`x_0 = 0`).

(no leak implies equal) Suppose the hypotenuse is never damaged and `OR(B_(t-1), C_t)` never differs. By induction on `t`, no outside cell is ever damaged. Case (i) is damaged iff that OR differs, since its permutive input is outside and undamaged. Cases (ii) and (iii) need a damaged hypotenuse cell. Cells further out read only undamaged outside cells. At time `n`, `T` is empty, so `F^n(uy) = F^n(vy)` for every `y`, and the sections agree by item 4. The hypotenuse and the OR test use cells of `T` only, so none of this depends on `y`.

(leak implies different) Otherwise take the first time `t_0` at which (i) fires, or at which the hypotenuse was damaged at `t_0 - 1`. Before `t_0`, no outside cell is damaged. If (i) fires, cell `f = n - t_0 + 1` is damaged at `t_0`. If the hypotenuse cell `n - t_0 + 1` was damaged at `t_0 - 1`, cell `n - t_0 + 2` becomes damaged at `t_0` iff cell `n - t_0 + 2` was 0 at `t_0 - 1`. That cell is outside, hence undamaged, and permutive in `x_(n+1) = y_1`, so choose `y_1` to make it 0; then `f = n - t_0 + 2`. (At `t_0 = n`, case (iii) damages cell 1 outright.)

Now force the front outward. At time `t`, let `f` be the outermost damaged cell, with everything beyond it undamaged. Cell `f + 1` is undamaged, and its outermost input `x_(f+1+t)` enters permutively. Here `f + 1 + t = n + 2` or `n + 3` at `t = t_0`, and the index rises by 2 per step, so it is always a `y` bit that no earlier choice depends on. Choose it to make cell `f + 1` equal 0. Then cell `f + 1` at `t + 1` reads `(0, 0, damaged)` through `x_(f+2) XOR (x_(f+1) OR x_f)` and is damaged, and everything beyond it reads undamaged cells only. By induction a damaged cell exists at time `n`, at position `<= 2n`, so the sections differ.

## Diagonal form of the signature, and a wall-free lower bound (U, C)

## Directional entropy: the trace conjecture follows from `h(Rule 30) > log 2` (U, C)

Eighth follow-up pass, 2026-09-19. Script `scripts/refute_knot-theory_trace_directional.py`, log `_trace_directional.log`, 45 s, exit 0. Pre-registration `scripts/refute_knot-theory_trace_directional.prereg.md`, written before the run. Target: move the entropy question onto Rule 30 itself. The conjecture stays CONJ.

**Reduction (U).** Put `R = sigma G`, where `(sigma y)_j = y_(j+1)`. Then `R(y)_j = y_(j+1) XOR (y_j OR y_(j-1))`, which is two-sided Rule 30 mirrored, so `h(R) = h(Rule 30)`. `G` commutes with `sigma`, so `R^2 = sigma^2 G^2 = (sigma^2 G) o G`. The directional entropy of a CA is homogeneous (Milnor 1988, Theorem 1, (3.5), p.364), so `h(T^2) = 2 h(T)`. It is **not** subadditive in general: Milnor's Example 6.4 (p.376) is a counterexample for topological entropy, and Hu (ETDS 1993) gives one for commuting measure-preserving maps (seen only through Luo–Zhao, arXiv:2203.16961). For a left- or right-permutive CA it is subadditive and continuous everywhere, for topological entropy and for every shift-invariant measure. The source is Milnor pp.378-379 (read): Lemma 6, `ĥ(p+q) <= ĥ(p)+ĥ(q)` whenever `p+q` lies in the dual cone, and the remark that for a left or right permutive block map "Lind and Smillie point out that the dual cones ... cover the vector space. Hence ĥ is subadditive and continuous everywhere". The statement concerns one CA's directional function on space-time vectors, which is the form used here. The remark carries no citation number. Smillie's LNM 1342 chapter (doi 10.1007/BFb0082855) was checked only through its Springer preview, which contains neither "subadditive" nor "permutive", so it is not cited for this. Lind, Physica D 10 (1984), is unread. `G` is permutive in `y_j`, its rightmost variable, so this applies. Hence `2 h(R) <= h(sigma^2 G) + h(G)`. `sigma^2 G` has neighbourhood `[0, 2]`, so `h(sigma^2 G) <= 2` bits. **Therefore `h(G) >= 2 (h(Rule 30) - 1)` bits.** This holds for topological entropy and for the uniform Bernoulli measure, which all three maps preserve. `h(Rule 30) >= 1` bit is the standard permutive bound. So the S3 trace conjecture follows from the strict inequality `h(Rule 30) > log 2`, in either sense. The implication runs one way only. `h(R) <= 1 + h(G)/2` is the same inequality rearranged, and `h(G) > 0` does not force `h(R) > 1`. So this is a stronger statement about Rule 30, not an easier one.

**Literature status (researcher pass, 2026-09-19).** Both statements are open. No source proves `h(Rule 30) = log 2` or `> log 2`, for either `h_top` or `h_mu`, and in that pass none gave a numerical estimate. Wolfram 1986 gives one, `h_mu ≲ 1.20`, with a footnote suggesting exactly 1 (read later; see the Wolfram paragraph below). Di Lena–Margara (JCA 2007, Cor. 4.6(2), read) reduce a permutive CA's `h_top` to its width-`2r+1` column subshift, which is a reduction, not a value. The nearest solved family is Coven (PAMS 1980): maps `x_0 + prod (x_i + b_i)` have `h = log 2` for aperiodic `b` (abstract only), and Liu–Ma (2016) handle periodic `b` (snippet only). These have exactly `G`'s shape, permutive at offset 0. `G = y_0 + 1 + (y_(-1) + 1)(y_(-2) + 1)` over GF(2), and its constant `1` survives complementation, so `G` is not in that family. Milnor 1988 (read) shows why every tool stops here. `G`'s direction is the permutive edge of Rule 30's light cone, where the XOR input moves at exactly light speed. That direction lies on the boundary of the dual cone, so the strict positivity of Lemma 8 (pp.379-380) does not reach it. Theorem 4's measure bound `h(v) >= |h.v|` (p.381) is 0 there. Milnor also flags a warning case (p.377). Coven's sibling map `x_0 + (1 - x_1) x_2` has positive topological entropy in every direction, yet behaves like a Class 2 map under the uniform measure. So along this very direction `h_top(G) > 0` and `h_mu(G) > 0` may have different answers.
