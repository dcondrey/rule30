## 1. Prepending lowers positive rank

Let `I(e)` be the inverse-terminal cut, `P` the zero-indexed Peel map, and
`sigma` the one-sided shift.  The rotated identity is

```text
P(I(sigma f)) = sigma^2 I(f).                        (1)
```

Suppose `e` is hard-core and `nu(I(e))=m>0`.  Prepend endpoint state `2` and
put `f=2e`, so `sigma f=e`.  State `2` can precede either hard-core symbol,
so `f` is still hard-core.  Equation (1) gives

```text
sigma^2 I(f) = P(I(e)).                              (2)
```

Eventual Peel rank is invariant under a finite temporal shift, while applying
`P` lowers a positive finite rank by one.  Hence

```text
nu(I(2e)) = nu(P(I(e))) = m-1.                       (3)
```

Iteration proves

```text
nu(I(2^m e)) = 0.                                    (4)
```

Therefore a hard-core endpoint with any finite inverse-cut rank exists if
and only if one exists at rank zero.  The previous positive-rank drift and
the possible unbounded dyadic period tower are no longer separate cases for
the exclusion problem.

## 2. Exact remaining separator

Rank zero means that `I(e)` is eventually zero, i.e. it is a nonzero
finite-support four-state cut.  The remaining theorem is simply

> **Rank-zero separator.** The terminal-cone image of no nonzero
> finite-support cut is a hard-core endpoint.

This statement would exclude all finite-rank accepted cuts by (4).  Every
cut generated by a finite zero-ray core has finite Peel rank, so it would
complete the nonconstant period-two exclusion.

This reformulation does not require a spectral-period classification.  It is
a finite-support boundary-collision theorem for the exact four-state
terminal cone.

## 4. Interaction with the lift monoid

For a periodic Peel output block `z`, cyclic Peel preimages are the fixed
points of its return transformation in the 13-element lift monoid.  The
output period doubles exactly for the three return transformations with a
two-cycle and no fixed point.  Equivalently, doubling occurs precisely at a
leaf of the fixed-period cyclic preimage tree.

This explains why dyadic scale can grow: it is not a phase accident.  The
fixed-period mortality basins already have maximum reverse depths `15` at
period `4` and `200` at period `8`; at period `16` an exact traversal exceeds
47,000 levels.  Thus a proof of (5) should use the finite-support terminal
boundary, not assert that period doubling eventually stops on abstract Peel
preimages.

## 5. Exact prefix grammar and the halving audit

For any endpoint symbol `q` and nonempty endpoint `e`, put `x=I(e)`.  The
rotated identity plus the first two inverse-cone cells gives the exact word

```text
I(qe) = (B(q), phi(q,x_0)) . P(x).                   (6)
```

The hard-core endpoint language has the disjoint recursive grammar

```text

```

Consequently (6) is a self-similar grammar for the complete accepted-cut

```text
P^n I(sigma^n e) = sigma^(2n) I(e) = q 0^omega,      (7)
```

Equations (6)-(7) redirect the proof search toward a recursive separator for
the two hard-core prefix branches, rather than a fixed suffix statistic.

## 6. First-infinite-tail reduction

There is a second uniform narrowing of the rank-zero case.  Put

```text
x^j = I(sigma^j e).
```

Suppose `x^0` is finite and nonzero.  The rotated identity gives

```text
P(x^(j+1)) = sigma^2 x^j.                            (8)
```

If both consecutive cuts are finite, the last-nonzero preservation rule
`phi(s,0)=3` for `s!=0` makes their last support positions differ by exactly
two.  Hence all `x^j` cannot remain finite: their nonnegative last-support
positions would decrease by two forever.

At the first index `j` for which `x^j` is infinite, equation (8) says that
`P(x^j)` is finite.  On its tail, therefore,

```text
x^j_(t+1) = g_0(x^j_t),
g_0 = (0,3,2,3).
```

The only eventual orbits of `g_0` are the fixed states `0`, `2`, and `3`
(state `1` enters `3`).  Eventual state `0` would make `x^j` finite,
contrary to the choice of `j`.  Thus:

> Every rank-zero hard-core counterexample has an endpoint tail whose
> inverse cut is eventually constant `2` or eventually constant `3`.

This is a strict reduction of the remaining acceptance language, but not yet
an exclusion.  The endpoint used after the finite-rank prefixing reduction
need not be a genuinely realizable right trace, so additional actual-right
forbidden factors cannot be imposed on this lemma without a separate
realizability argument.
