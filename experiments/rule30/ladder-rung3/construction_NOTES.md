# Construction probe: substitutions fail, but a7's stated i.o. lemma follows

**Attribution correction:** the five-zero theorem and all forbidden factors
used here were already proved/recorded in
`../p1-period2-invariant/RESULTS-RIGHT-FILTERED-MORTALITY.md` sections 1–2
and `right_trace_forbidden.py`. They are reused prior results, not new
discoveries. This file retains the independent replay details; the concise
final report is `construction_report.md`.

This records the independent Direction A probe. It does **not** produce an
aperiodic Rule30 diagram. It proves a narrower, uniform fact about every
alternating-centre diagram, sufficient for the actual odd-q lemma in a7.

The registered mechanism was to prescribe an explicitly aperiodic sequence
`a_n = col_1(T0+2n)`, at times `col_0=0`, using Fibonacci or zero-interleaved
Thue–Morse. The registered kill was a finite forbidden factor under the exact
forward rule, checked before any attempt to extrapolate feasible prefixes.
The search cap was factor length 16; both candidates died earlier.

## Uniform zero-run bound (`U`, with finite certificates `C`)

Normalize the alternating onset to time 0, so `col_0(t)=t mod 2`. At an even
time let the first five right-hand cells be `(a,b,c,d,e)`. Two forward steps
under the fixed boundary give

```
a' = NOT(a OR b OR c).
```

For triples starting in zero, the complete transition facts are

```
000 -> 1**
001 -> 010
010 -> 000
011 -> 00(d OR e).
```

These are identities, with arbitrary cells further right. They follow by
substituting twice in `l XOR (m OR r)`; the probe also evaluates all 32 possible
five-bit inputs through the existing `torus.FWD[30]` and verifies them.
Consequently the longest chain retaining first bit zero is

```
011 -> 001 -> 010 -> 000 -> 1**.
```

Thus `a_n` cannot contain five consecutive zeroes. Equivalently, every five
consecutive times in the centre's zero set include a time with `col_1=1`.
This proves a7's **stated** i.o. lemma for `X(k,01)` for every `k`, because its
centre alternates from the proved onset `2k+1`. It does not establish the
additional even-q condition or non-eventual-periodicity.

The four-zero indicator is exactly

```
P(a_0 a_1 a_2 a_3 = 0000)
  = (NOT x1) AND x2 AND x3 AND (x4 OR x5),
```

independent of `x6,x7`, where `x1,x2,...` are the right-hand cells at the first
centre-zero time. The audit checks this over all 128 initial words of width 7:
12 satisfy it. Of all 512 words of width 9, 48 begin with four zero observations,
and every one gives the observation word `00001`. These are exhaustive truth
identities for the exact finite light cones, hence uniform statements at every
time, not estimates from a temporal simulation.

The existing `T=10` torus, built from `U=0101010101`, `V=1101000100`, has
`a=10000` repeated. It is forward-verified at all cells using existing code;
its spatial period is 155. Thus the five-observation bound is sharp.

The locality filter is respected: the proof establishes a finite forbidden
word and consequent recurrence, **not** a finite-window decision of eventual
periodicity. The period-10 example also demonstrates why this recurrence
cannot settle the aperiodicity question.

## Explicit killed aperiodic prescriptions (`K`, `C`)

The Fibonacci fixed point is generated by `0 -> 01, 1 -> 0`, starting from 0.
The Thue–Morse fixed point is generated by `0 -> 01, 1 -> 10`, with coding
`b -> b0` afterward. Both coded sources have no consecutive ones, so they pass
the immediate `a_n a_{n+1}=0` necessary condition.

| prescribed source | shortest forbidden factor | first occurrence | all initial rows independently exhausted | matches |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Fibonacci | `101001` | 4 | `2^11 = 2048` | 0 |
| zero-interleaved Thue–Morse | `00000` | 9 | `2^9 = 512` | 0 |

Factor lengths preceding the kill have these exact feasible factor counts:
Fibonacci lengths 1–5: `2,3,4,5,6`; Thue–Morse lengths 1–4: `2,3,5,7`.
At the killing lengths there are respectively 7 and 10 source factors, each
with exactly one infeasible factor. All smaller factors were checked SAT and
the returned initial rows were independently replayed with `torus.FWD[30]`.

The factor lists are complete, not sampled: expand the substitution until
each supertile has length at least `m-1`, then enumerate length-m factors of
every allowed adjacent pair of supertiles. Every length-m factor spans at
most two such supertiles. Fibonacci's pair set is exactly `{00,01,10}`;
Thue–Morse's is all four pairs, followed by the coding. The exact lists also
agree with the factors in the first 2048 source symbols.

The shortest infeasible Fibonacci **prefix** is `0100101`, length 7, verified
by UNSAT and independent exhaustion of all `2^13 = 8192` initial rows. The
shortest infeasible coded Thue–Morse prefix is `00101000100000`, length 14;
its infeasibility was checked by SAT and already follows from the embedded
forbidden word `00000`. The shorter prefixes all returned satisfying models
replayed through the unmodified forward rule.

These deaths do not eliminate all substitution constructions; they identify
why the two named natural constructions do not extend to a Rule30 diagram.

## Rule 90 and code scope

`construction_substitution_probe.py` imports the existing Rule90 control
`controls.rule90_control`, which reports bijectivity at `T=2,4` and valid
full-period tori. The proof above explicitly uses OR: without it the first
right-cell two-step formula is `a'=1 XOR a XOR c`, so the zero-run transition
chain does not apply. Rule90's existing inverse-column bijection leaves its
zero-set trace unrestricted.

The finite-cone SAT encoding has one free initial bit for each cell in the
exact dependency cone, fixed centre boundary `t mod 2`, and no other boundary
condition. For m observations it uses right initial cells `1..2m-1` and
observations at `t=0,2,...,2m-2`. It encodes the same forward formula as
`torus.FWD[30]`; SAT models are independently replayed. UNSAT obstruction
certificates above are also replayed by exhaustive scalar initial-row checks.

Existing experiment files are unmodified. The results JSON records their
SHA256 hashes. The construction probe alone does not establish acceptance by
`ladder.decide`; no full aperiodic candidate survived to that stage.
