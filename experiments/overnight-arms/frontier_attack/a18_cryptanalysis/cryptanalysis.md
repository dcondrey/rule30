# NO BIAS FOUND

Four independent standard cryptanalytic bias-detection techniques, applied to the
lone-seed Rule 30 centre column (OEIS A051023) to depth `T = 2^20 = 1,048,576`
bits -- with the two depth-sensitive tests confirmed again at
`T = 2^21 = 2,097,152`, past register row 8's `T >= 2,000,000` -- produce **no
distinguisher from an i.i.d. Bernoulli(1/2) keystream**.  Over
a pre-registered ledger of `N = 100,838` hypotheses the smallest raw p-value is
`6.214e-6`, which is `0.627` after Bonferroni correction; Benjamini-Hochberg at
`q = 0.05` rejects nothing.  **This is a statement about a finite prefix: finite
data, however deep, bounds a test's power at that depth and can never establish
randomness, absence of structure, or nonperiodicity as an infinite statement
(obstruction H).**

The single hypothesis that crossed the Bonferroni line on first pass was audited
and shown to be an artifact of a normal approximation applied to a lattice-valued
statistic; see section 5.  All four tests are shown to have detection power by
controls that they do detect.

Status: **MEASURED, diagnostic only.**  Contributes nothing to P1, P2 or P3 in
either direction.  Section 8 states what a reader must not over-read.

## 1. What Meier-Staffelbach shows, and why it is a different claim

Rule 30 is Mathematica's historical default RNG and has been attacked as a stream
cipher.  The canonical attack is Meier & Staffelbach, *Analysis of Pseudo Random
Sequences Generated by Cellular Automata*, EUROCRYPT 1991, LNCS 547, 186-199.
Two claims are routinely conflated and they are not the same claim.

**What M&S actually attack: STATE RECOVERY.**  Wolfram's cipher runs rule 30 on a
finite **cyclic ring of `n` cells** with a **secret initial configuration** (the
key) and XORs the centre cell's temporal sequence into the plaintext.  M&S give a
known-plaintext attack -- equivalently known-keystream, since XOR keystream makes
the two coincide -- that recovers that initial configuration.  The mechanism is
**left-permutivity**: rule 30 is `s(t+1,x) = s(t,x-1) XOR (s(t,x) OR s(t,x+1))`,
so `s(t,x-1) = s(t+1,x) XOR (s(t,x) OR s(t,x+1))` and the left neighbour is
determined by the output plus the centre and right cells.  Guess the `floor(n/2)`
bits of the right half of the seed, evolve forward to get the right-adjacent
temporal sequence, then solve the left triangle backward.  It needs about
`ceil(n/2)` bits of keystream.  The naive cost is `2^floor(n/2)`, but M&S's own
contribution is that the right halves are **not equiprobable**, which cuts it far
below that: for `n = 300`, about **18.1 bits of entropy** suffice to recover the
seed with probability 0.5.  M&S conclude `n` must be about 1000 bits for security.

**What M&S do NOT show: a statistical distinguisher.**  Their analysis is key
search and an information-theoretic treatment of non-uniform key sources.  The
weakness is that the keystream is *invertible given enough of it*, not that it is
*distinguishable from random*.  Those are independent properties: a sequence can
be perfectly invertible and still pass every statistical test, which is exactly
rule 30's situation.  The affirmative evidence runs the other way.  Len et al.
(2003) subjected every uniform ECA's centre-cell stream to frequency, serial,
poker, gap and autocorrelation tests; rule 30 passed, and passed the subsequent
linear-complexity test with `L` approaching the ideal `l/2` -- **and was still
state-recoverable** by an adapted M&S algorithm.  Wolfram states the same
directly: "I've tried all sorts of statistical randomness tests on the center
column of rule 30 -- and never found any significant deviation from randomness"
(*Announcing the Rule 30 Prizes*, 2019), with one billion steps computed
(500,025,038 black against 499,974,962 white).

**So: "Rule 30 is a weak cipher" and "the lone-seed trace has detectable bias"
are different claims.  The first is established.  The second is what this arm
tests, and this arm does not find it.**

Three further scope differences between M&S's object and this one.  M&S attack a
**finite cyclic ring** with a **secret** seed; this arm studies the **infinite
lattice** with the **single known** seed `s(0,x) = [x=0]`.  M&S consume `n/2`
output bits to recover `n` unknowns; here there are no unknowns to recover.  And
a state-recovery attack is trivial here for a reason that says nothing about
randomness: the initial condition is public.

*Retrieval caveat, recorded rather than hidden.*  The body text of M&S 1991 could
not be obtained (Springer paywall; proxy renderings return abstract and reference
list only).  The mechanism, the cyclic-ring precondition, the `ceil(n/2)`
keystream requirement and the 18.1-bit figure are taken from Spencer,
*Cellular Automata in Cryptographic Random Generators*, arXiv:1306.3546, §2.2 and
§3.2, which restates the algorithm in detail with a worked example; the key-search
framing and the "keys are not equiprobable" and "certain success probability"
clauses are verbatim from the M&S abstract.  M&S's reference list includes
Maurer's *A Universal Statistical Test for Random Bit Generators* (Crypto'90), so
a statistical-test discussion inside the unread body cannot be fully excluded; my
reading is that it is cited for entropy estimation of the key source, and that is
inference, not documentation.

## 2. What this arm adds over register row 8

Row 8 (`RESULTS-orbit-closure-diagnostic.md`) ran a **block-frequency** test: are
the `2^L` blocks of length `L` equally frequent in windows of width `W`, to
`T >= 2,000,000`?  It found deviations following the `W^-1/2` law inside an
i.i.d. Bernoulli band.  Block frequency is a first-moment test.  It is blind to
every structure that preserves block counts, and every technique below is chosen
to see something block frequency cannot:

* **linear complexity profile** sees a short linear recurrence, which does not
  perturb block frequencies at all;
* **the correlation-attack statistic** sees a predictive relation between a
  window and a *distant* bit, which is a joint property across a lag;
* **NIST's runs, longest-run, cumulative-sums, approximate-entropy, serial and
  spectral tests** see run structure, random-walk excursion, conditional entropy
  and periodicity;
* **autocorrelation** sees a lag-`l` shift relation.

This arm is consistent with row 8 rather than independent of it, and it is the
cryptanalytic-technique complement rather than a deeper block-frequency run.

**Distinctness from the other two rows the task flagged.**

*Row 14 (Arm 4, AIR/frequency-domain)* already measured "BM linear complexity
maximal through 16384" as one of three disconfirming measurements.  That result is
taken as given and is **not re-run as this arm's claim**.  A maximal ceiling does
not determine the *profile*: a sequence can sit on the `n/2` line while its jump
structure is anomalous.  Test 1 therefore measures jump count `J(n)`, mean jump
height `Hbar(n)` and the accumulated deviation `G(n)`, none of which the ceiling
fixes -- and extends the depth from `2^14` to `2^20`.

*Row 43 (ANF/Walsh of `f_t`)* analysed the iterated centre-bit **function** over
all `2^(2t+1)` inputs -- obstruction G territory.  Test 2 measures correlation
along the **single actual orbit** of the lone seed, a measure-zero subset of that
input space, and it is the orbit that P1 and P2 are statements about.

## 3. Setup, gate, and the sequences under test

Everything below was pre-registered in `PREREG.md`, frozen before any data was
generated.  Deviations are listed in section 7 and were not made by editing the
pre-registration.

**Gate.**  The centre column is generated by the shifted-frame bigint recurrence
`b <- (b<<2) XOR ((b<<1) | b)` and checked bit-for-bit over its first `65,536`
bits against **both** `experiments/rule30/center_column.py` (the repo's
OEIS-gated ground truth) and
`experiments/overnight-arms/common/rule30.py::center_column_bits`.  Both:
**MATCH**.  No statistic runs unless both gates pass.  Density at `T = 2^20`:
`524,976 / 1,048,576 = 0.500656`.

| id | sequence | role |
|---|---|---|
| `rule30` | lone-seed Rule 30 centre column | the object |
| `rule90` | lone-seed Rule 90 centre column (`1,0,0,...`) | rule filter |
| `lfsr32` | maximal-length degree-32 LFSR keystream | power control, test 1 |
| `bern51` | i.i.d. Bernoulli(0.51) | power control, test 3 |
| `copy1000` | `y_t = y_{t-1000}` w.p. 0.02, else fresh fair bit | power control, tests 2 and 4 |
| `lag1000` | `r_t XOR r_{t-1000}` | mis-specified control, see section 7 |
| `iid_00..19` | i.i.d. Bernoulli(1/2) | null band, operative threshold |

**Why Rule 90 alone is not enough as a control.**  Its column is `1` then zeros
forever.  A test that trips on it has detected an all-zero string, which most
tests do trivially; that establishes almost nothing about sensitivity to
*subtle* structure.  So three non-degenerate controls were registered, each
naming in advance the test it must break, and all three do (section 6).

**Multiple-comparison correction.**  `N = 18 + 780 + 40 + 100,000 = 100,838`,
fixed before data.  Bonferroni at family-wise `alpha = 0.05` gives a
per-hypothesis threshold of `4.959e-7` (`|z| >= 5.032`); this is the headline.
Benjamini-Hochberg FDR at `q = 0.05` is reported alongside because Bonferroni over
`10^5` lags is very conservative.  Raw p-values for every hypothesis are in the
JSON outputs: all 780 test-2 rows per sequence (`all_rows`), all 10 x 100
per-block NIST p-values per sequence (`per_block_pvalues`), and the full
100,000-lag `z` array for rule30 (`test4_rule30_z.npy`).

*On padding.*  `combine.py` carries the 779 non-extreme test-2 hypotheses and, for
control sequences, the 99,999 non-extreme test-4 lags at `p = 1.0` rather than
enumerating them, and asserts the ledger is exactly `100,838` long for all six
sequences.  This is conservative for both corrections and cannot manufacture a
rejection: only the minimum p can survive Bonferroni, and for BH, raising values
to the maximum can only push surviving real p-values to lower ranks where
`q k / N` is smaller.  rule30's test-4 family uses the exact 100,000-value array,
not padding.

### Test 2 — correlation-attack statistic along the orbit
`test2_correlation_attack.py`, `test2b_power_check.py`

`eps(k, Delta) = (1/2) sum_w |Pr[w, y=1] - Pr[w, y=0]|`, the correlation attained
by the maximum-likelihood Boolean function `g` predicting `c_{t+Delta}` from the
window `(c_t, ..., c_{t+k-1})`.  `k in {1,2,3,4,6,8}`, `Delta` over
`{k..128} U {256,512,1024,2048,4096}`; ledger 780, matching the registered count.
`Delta >= k` is asserted in code: `c_{t+Delta}` is *determined* by row-`t` cells
in `[-Delta, Delta]` and the window overlaps the target when `Delta < k`, which
would return `eps = 1/2` by construction and would be measuring the dynamics
rather than a bias.

Rule 30's extreme is `eps = 0.001953` at `k = 3, Delta = 38`, `z = 3.062` against
the pooled i.i.d. null.  **0 of 780** survive Bonferroni; **0 of 780** exceed even
the uncorrected i.i.d. seed max, which is the operative threshold under the row 8
convention.  At this depth, no small local guess function predicts a later centre
bit better than chance (obstruction H).

### Test 3 — NIST SP 800-22, block protocol
`test3_nist.py`

`K = 100` disjoint blocks of `10^4` bits, one p-value per subtest per block, then
NIST's two meta-tests: pass proportion at `alpha = 0.01` inside
`[0.9602, 1.0]`, and uniformity of the 100 p-values by chi-square on 10 bins
(`P_T >= 0.0001`) plus a KS test.

*The DFT variance constant, corrected from an earlier draft of this document.*
An earlier draft called `sqrt(n * 0.95 * 0.05 / 4)` "the corrected constant" and
said the 2001 document's was wrong.  That is backwards: `d = 4` **is** the
SP 800-22 (2001) constant, and `d = 3.8` is the Kim-Umeno-Hasegawa (2004)
correction to it.  Rather than fix the citation and move on,
`null_calibration.py` runs all four variants (`d in {4, 3.8}` x DC bin
included/dropped) over 2,000 pure-null blocks.  All four give mean p `~ 0.50` and
`frac(p < 0.01) ~ 0.008` against the nominal `0.01`, and all four show the same
mild non-uniformity (KS p from `2.0e-5` to `2.4e-4`).  The departure is therefore
**not** the constant or the DC bin -- swapping either barely moves it.  It is the
discreteness of the statistic: `n1` is an integer count with s.d. `~10.9` at
`n = 10^4`, so the p-values live on a visible lattice and a KS test on 2,000
samples has the power to see it.  At the level the uniformity meta-test is
actually applied -- 100 blocks per sequence -- exactly **1 of 20** pure-null seeds
fails it, the expected rate.  rule30's DFT verdict is unaffected.

## 5. The one hit, and why it is an artifact

On first pass, test 1 reported rule30 at `|z| = 5.42` on `D(65536)`, the mean
signed deviation of the linear complexity profile from the `m/2` line, against a
20-seed normal z-score.  That crosses `5.032`.  It was audited before being
reported and it does not survive.  `test1_bm_profile_output.txt` still records
its own pre-audit verdict, `REJECT (profile anomalous)`, and that line is left
standing deliberately: the audit below is what overturns it, and a reader should
be able to see both.  `test1b_bm_null_mc.py`:

1. **`D` is lattice-valued and its null is nearly a point mass at exactly `1/4`.**
   Rueppel gives `E[L_m - m/2] = (4 + (m mod 2))/18`, averaging to exactly `1/4`,
   and 17 to 19 of the 20 null seeds return the single value `0.25` exactly.
   Across 500 seeds, `G(65536)` takes only **4 distinct values**.  A normal
   approximation on a statistic like that is not valid, and the 20-sample s.d. of
   `1.5e-5` that produced the `z` is an artifact of the near-degeneracy, not a
   scale.  The z was also built on a 20-sample s.d., so it is a `t_19` quantity
   even for a well-behaved statistic.
2. **Non-parametric null, 500 seeds, exact integer statistic.**  rule30's
   `G(65536) = 32,756`; the 500-seed null range is `[32,744, 32,768]`.  Inside.
   Empirical two-sided `p = 0.064`, and `p = 1.000` at all eight other prefixes.
3. **Replication across depth.**  A structural bias persists or grows with `n`;
   a fluctuation does not.  `G(n) - median_null` is exactly `0` at
   `n = 2^10 .. 2^15`, `-12` at `n = 2^16`, and back to exactly `0` at `2^17` and
   `2^18`.  It does not replicate.

`D` is retired as a z-scored statistic and `G` with an empirical null replaces it
in the combined ledger.  The empirical p-value floor on 500 seeds is `1/501`,
which is 4,000x the Bonferroni threshold, so **test 1 as run can refute a hit but
cannot by itself confirm one**.  That is stated as a power limit, not a pass.

## 7. Deviations from the pre-registration

Both are recorded here; `PREREG.md` above this line was not edited.

**D1. The `lag1000` power control was mis-specified and is provably null.**
`y_t = r_t XOR r_{t-1000}` was registered as having a planted lag-1000
correlation.  It does not: `y_t XOR y_{t+1000} = r_{t-1000} XOR r_{t+1000}`, which
is uniform, so the construction destroys the very correlation it was meant to
plant.  It is retained in every table as an instructive failure and correctly
returns NO BIAS FOUND.  `copy1000` (a copy channel: `y_t = y_{t-1000}` with
probability `0.02`, else a fresh fair bit, giving lag-1000 autocorrelation exactly
`0.02` and `z ~ 20`) was added as the corrected control before test 4 was
re-scored.

**D2. The registered test-2 lag grid does not contain `Delta = 1000`.**
`copy1000` was registered to fire on tests 2 and 4.  It fired on test 4 and not on
test 2 -- because the grid is `{k..128} U {256,512,1024,2048,4096}` and the
planted correlation sits in a gap of it, not because test 2 lacks power.  That
distinction is only credible if demonstrated, so `test2b_power_check.py` evaluates
`eps(k, Delta = 1000)` directly: `copy1000` reaches `z = 42.8`, rule30 reaches
`z = 0.47`.  This supplementary check is reported **outside** the `N = 100,838`
ledger and is not folded into it, because it was chosen after seeing test 2's
output; adding it would be exactly the multiple-comparison abuse the
pre-registration exists to prevent.

**D3. Test 1's ledger is not the one that was registered, and the matching count
of 18 is a coincidence.**  PREREG registered test 1's 18 hypotheses as
`{J, Hbar, D}` x 6 prefix lengths.  The delivered ledger is `{G, J}` x 9
prefixes, which is also 18 by accident, not by design.  Three separate changes:
`D` was replaced by the exact integer `G` with an empirical null (section 5
explains why, and that substitution is the whole point of the audit); `Hbar` was
dropped from the correction entirely and now appears only as a descriptive column
in the section 4 table; and the prefix set changed because test1b's null runs on
powers of two from `2^10` to `2^18`.  `Hbar`'s removal was not deliberate and is
disclosed here rather than defended: it tracks `J` very closely (both are
functions of the same jump list) so it carried little independent information,
but that is a post-hoc justification and the honest statement is that it was
dropped in the rewrite.  `Hbar` is within the i.i.d. seed band at every prefix
for rule30 in `test1_bm_profile_output.txt`, so restoring it would not change any
verdict.

**D4. Seven of SP 800-22's 15 tests were not implemented.**  Named with reasons,
per success criterion (b), in `test3_nist_output.txt` and in
`test3_nist.py::NOT_IMPLEMENTED`: binary matrix rank (a `10^4`-bit block gives
~9 of the 38 matrices NIST needs for a valid chi-square), non-overlapping template
matching (148 templates x 100 blocks would add ~14,800 hypotheses to a ledger
fixed before data), overlapping template matching (same, plus it needs the `m = 9`
Pi-table at `n = 10^6` per block), Maurer's universal test (needs
`n >= 387,840` for `L = 6`, impossible at this block size), random excursions and
random excursions variant (both need `>= 500` cumulative-sum cycles, which a
`10^4`-bit block does not supply, so NIST's own applicability condition fails),
and NIST's linear-complexity test, which was **deliberately** omitted as redundant
with test 1 -- test 1 measures the Berlekamp-Massey profile directly to `n = 2^20`
against a 500-seed non-parametric null, where NIST's version is a coarser
chi-square on jump classes over short blocks.

**Not a deviation, recorded for completeness:** test 1 ran far faster than
budgeted (1.4 s to `n = 2^18`), so the depth was extended from the registered
`2^18` to `2^20` for rule30's `G(n)`, and the null was strengthened from the
registered 20 seeds to 500.  Both changes make the test harder to pass, not
easier.

## 8. Scope: what a reader must not over-read

**Obstruction H, restated because it governs every number above.**  Every result
here is a statement about a finite prefix: `2^20` bits for the pre-registered
ledger, `2^21` for the two depth-sensitive tests.  A finite prefix, however deep,
bounds a statistical test's power at that depth and can never establish
randomness, absence of structure, or nonperiodicity as an infinite statement.
"No bias found to `T = 2^20` (`2^21` for tests 2 and 4) by these four techniques
at these thresholds" is the entire claim.  The depth reached is roughly `2^-9` of
Wolfram's own `10^9`-bit check, which itself proves nothing infinite.  A period beginning at `10^12` is untouched by
any of this, exactly as the prize announcement's own `10^9`-bit check cannot
exclude a trillion-step transient.

**The single-column gate (PATH.md 0.1) is passed trivially and that is not a
credential.**  Section 0.1 retires quantities that move by `O(1/W)` when column 0
is overwritten by a periodic word.  Every statistic here is a functional of column
0 *alone*, so the overwrite replaces the input entirely rather than perturbing it.
`single_column_gate.py` measures it rather than asserting it: overwriting column 0
with `0101...` or `0110100...` moves six of the seven headline statistics by
`>= 0.82` relative (linear complexity ratio and BM jump count by `1.000`,
autocorrelation max by `0.997`, `eps(1,1)` by `0.999`, approximate-entropy p by
`1.000`).  The seventh, density, moves by only `0.001` under the period-2 word --
because that word has density exactly `1/2`, a coincidence of the probe, not
insensitivity; under the period-7 word it moves by `0.144`.  This arm **is** the
column, so the gate is not a live constraint on it.  Passing 0.1 says only that
the statistic reads column 0; it says nothing about whether the statistic can
decide anything.

**This does not bear on P1, P2 or P3.**  A clean statistical null is not evidence
of nonperiodicity (P1): an eventually periodic sequence with a `10^12` transient
would produce these same tables.  It is not evidence for equidistribution (P2):
row 8 already covers the frequency question and this arm adds no bound.  It is
irrelevant to P3.

**This is validation, not discovery.**  Wolfram reports no significant deviation
from randomness on the centre column to `10^9` steps, and Len et al. (2003) report
rule 30 passing frequency, serial, poker, gap and autocorrelation tests with
near-ideal linear complexity.  The expected outcome was that nothing survives, and
nothing did.  What is contributed is precision, not a new direction: a
pre-registered ledger of `100,838` hypotheses with a stated global correction, an
audited artifact, and four power controls that each name the test they break --
in place of "it passes the tests."

**A negative result at this depth also has a cost.**  It closes the cheap
empirical route to a distinguisher.  If the lone-seed column has cryptanalytic
structure, it is not of a kind that linear complexity, low-order orbit
correlations, the NIST battery or autocorrelation can see at `2^20` bits, and a
deeper run of the same statistics is unlikely to be the thing that finds it.
