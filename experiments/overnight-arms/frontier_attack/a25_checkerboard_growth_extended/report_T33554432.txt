## Q4 how much further would K alone need to go

  Reference law: a checkerboard (2k+1) x k patch costs 2^-(2k+1) for its
  first row and 2^-(k-1) per further row, so K advances by 1 per 8x in T
  (3k <= log2 T).  a3 states the same thing as 'K=20 needs T ~ 2^60'.
  dK/d(doubling of T) = 1/3 for the checkerboards, 1/6 for all-zeros
  (per-row factor 4 -> 2k+1+2(k-1) <= log2 T).

  K is integer-valued and moves in steps of 1.  Under the growth
  hypothesis its increments over D doublings are ~Poisson with mean
  s*D.  To make 'K did not move at all' improbable (P < 0.05) needs
  s*D > 3, i.e. D > 3/s doublings.  Generation is Theta(T^2) with the
  constant fitted from this arm's own C-kernel checkpoints, 2.29e-11 s per step^2.

  family       s=dK/doubling   D needed   T needed      single-core kernel time
  all_zeros          0.167         18   8.796e+12       5.61e+07 core-years
  checker_A          0.333          9   1.718e+10            214 core-years
  checker_B          0.333          9   1.718e+10            214 core-years

  So the diagonal K cannot be made decisive by any horizon: the cost
  of one more unit of K is 8x the horizon and therefore 64x the
  compute, and several units are needed before 'flat' is excluded.
  Cost of the next few units of K from this arm's horizon:
    +dK   T needed      kernel time
    +1    2.684e+08          19.09 days
    +2    2.147e+09           1222 days
    +3    1.718e+10       7.82e+04 days

  The rate statistic is what makes the question answerable at a
  horizon that fits in hours: it uses up to 1e6 occurrences per cell
  instead of one maximum, so its precision improves as sqrt(T) rather
  than log(T).
