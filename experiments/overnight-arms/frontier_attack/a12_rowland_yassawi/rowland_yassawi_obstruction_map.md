## 2. Rule 30's structural profile, fixed once

`s(t+1,x) = s(t,x-1) XOR (s(t,x) OR s(t,x+1))`; over `F_2`,
`F(x)_i = x_{i-1} + x_i + x_{i+1} + x_i x_{i+1}`; neighbourhood `[-1,1]`,

| property | Rule 30 | witness |
|---|---|---|
| left-permutive | **yes** | `a ↦ a XOR c` bijective for every `c` |
| right-permutive | **no** | `r(1,1,0) = 1 XOR 1 = 0` and `r(1,1,1) = 1 XOR 1 = 0`, so `r(1,1,·)` is constant |

| algebraic (`F` a group endomorphism of `A^Z`) | **no** | the `x_i x_{i+1}` term breaks additivity: `F(x+y) ≠ F(x)+F(y)` |

### 3.3 Sablik, ETDS 27:1965-1990 (2007) — VERIFIED from arXiv math/0510564v1

*Mathieu Sablik, "Measure rigidity for algebraic bipermutative cellular
automata", arXiv:math/0510564 [math.DS] v1 26 Oct 2005; ETDS 27 (2007) 1965-1990,
DOI 10.1017/S0143385707000247 (vol/pages/DOI from Semantic Scholar; Cambridge

**(a) Hypotheses, verbatim.**

### 3.4 Tal, arXiv:2604.10124 — VERIFIED, and it carries the one live row

*Matan Tal (Einstein Institute of Mathematics, HUJI), "On a Constraint on

**(a) Hypotheses, verbatim.**

**Definition 1.1**: "Let τ : Λ^N₀ → Λ^N₀ be a cellular automaton defined as

concludes uniformity on a right coset; its proof opens "Since τ_G is
bi-permutative, Theorem 4.5 applies here."

**(b) Consumption.** From the proof of 4.5, verbatim, with both halves of
bipermutativity spent separately:

> "The left permutativity of τ guarantees that the conditions of Lemma 4.1 are
> fulfilled for σ, τ in place of S, T respectively. Thus x ↦ μₓ^(σ⁻¹ℬ)({x}) is
> τ-invariant. By Lemma 4.3, the function … is measurable relative to the Pinsker
> σ-algebra of τ. By this and by the right permutativity of τ, applying Lemma
> 4.4, … is also measurable relative to the Pinsker σ-algebra of σ since it is
> the same."

**Lemma 4.4** is the exact consumption point of right-permutivity: "Let τ be a
right permutative cellular automaton. If μ is a Borel probability measure
invariant under σ and τ then the Pinsker σ-algebra of σ is equal to that of τ."
Its proof: "∨_{i=0}^{N-1} σ_{(1,0)}^{-i} ξ = ∨_{i=0}^{N-1} σ_{(0,1)}^{-i} ξ by
the right permutativity of τ."

**(c) Rule 30 and the minimal weakening — Tal supplies the weakening in print,
and it does not reach Rule 30.**

Rule 30 fails right-permutivity, so Thm. 4.5 fails at Lemma 4.4. §7 replaces
bipermutativity with the weakest condition anyone has published:

> **Definition 7.7.** We shall say that a two-dimensional subshift W ⊆ Λ^(N₀²) is
> an *RLP subshift* if it is reciprocal and the almost-generation by (1,0) is
> done in a left permutative manner.

*Reciprocal* (Def. 7.5) requires almost-generation by **both** `(1,0)` and
`(0,1)`. **Theorem 7.8** is Thm. 4.5 for RLP subshifts, with **Lemma 7.6**
replacing Lemma 4.4. This is the minimal weakening in the literature, and Rule 30
is not known to satisfy it, because reciprocity is exactly the `(0,1)`-direction
generation that a non-right-permutive rule does not obviously give. The paper
says so:

> (p. 33) "Apart from this example, its generalization (to higher dimensional
> tori etc.) and bi-permutative cellular automata, there are no other proven
> examples of RLP subshifts in our disposal. Examining whether subshifts satisfy
> the reciprocity requirement seems a challenging task."

**And the paper's own open problem confirms that measure rigidity is not
established even inside the bipermutative class:**

> **Open Problem** (pp. 4-5). "Are there cellular automata, other than the ones
> originating from symbolic codings of the times 2 times 3 map on the circle and
> its generalizations, for which the uniform measure is the only shift-invariant
> measure that is also invariant relative to the automaton ergodic relative to
> the joint action of the shift and the automaton that possesses positive entropy
> relative to each of the two maps? Maybe some requirement on RLP subshifts can
> guarantee this property?"

**The one statement in the whole map whose structural hypothesis Rule 30
satisfies — Tal Theorem 3.2:**

> **Theorem 3.2.** "Let τ : Λ^N₀ → Λ^N₀ be a left permutative cellular automaton,
> and let μ be an ergodic τ-invariant Borel probability measure on Λ^N₀ … Then
> there exists 1 ≤ k_μ ≤ |Λ| for which μ̃ₓ^(σ_{(1,0)}^{-1}ℬ̃)({x}) = 1/k_μ
> μ̃-almost-surely."

Tal attributes this to Pivato ("appears in [7] as Theorem 4.1", [7] = Pivato DCDS
12(4):723-736). **Unresolved discrepancy, flagged not resolved:** the verified
arXiv v1 of Pivato (math/0306211) contains no left-permutive-only theorem that
this arm located — its Thm. 22 requires a quasigroup CA — and the DCDS printed
numbering is unverified (§3.2). Either DCDS renumbers and restates, or the
attribution is loose. **Do not cite "Pivato Thm. 4.1" for a left-permutive-only
statement without reading the DCDS print.**

Taking Thm. 3.2 at Tal's statement of it, this is the row that matters, and it
lands on the wrong side of obstruction E for a reason that is now precise:
**it requires an ergodic τ-invariant measure to be supplied in advance.** For
R8's `Y`, supplying such a measure is the whole problem. Its conclusion is a
μ-a.e. property of conditional measures on a two-dimensional extension. It says
nothing about any single orbit, and nothing about the value `1/2`.

*Radius caveat, honestly recorded:* Def. 1.1 is stated for `r : Λ² → Λ`
(radius 1, two variables). Rule 30's local rule has three variables, so it is not
literally a Def. 1.1 instance, and the naive block recoding `y_i = (x_i,x_{i+1})`
gives radius 2, not radius 1. A conjugacy was not verified. "Rule 30 is a Def.
1.1 CA" is therefore **inferred, not documented**. The permutivity verdict does
not depend on it: Def. 7.3's left-permutive-manner condition carries no radius
restriction and Rule 30 satisfies it.

**(T) No / unknown.** §§4-6 classify `(σ,τ)`-invariant measures on `Λ^N₀`. §7's
RLP objects *are* two-dimensional subshifts, so §7 is the only entry in this map

unknown and is not settled here.

**(E) Wrong side.** Every conclusion is μ-a.e.: Thms. 4.5, 5.1, 7.8, 7.14 all

### 3.5 Rowland-Yassawi, CJM 72(6):1691-1726 (2020) — VERIFIED from arXiv:1811.01256v3

*Eric Rowland, Reem Yassawi, "Automaticity and Invariant Measures of Linear
Cellular Automata", Canadian J. Math. 72(6):1691-1726 (2020), DOI
10.4153/S0008414X19000488, arXiv:1811.01256 (v1 3 Nov 2018, v3 19 Feb 2020).
Journal page = arXiv page + 1690. Register's "CJM 72 (2020) 1691" is correct.*

**(a) Hypothesis.** **Definition 2.2** (arXiv p. 4): "A cellular automaton
Φ : F_p^Z → F_p^Z is *linear* if Φ is an F_p-linear map, i.e. (Φ(u))_m =
α_{−ℓ}u_{m−ℓ} + · · · + α_0 u_m + · · · + α_r u_{m+r} …" with generating
polynomial `φ(x) = α_{−ℓ}x^ℓ + · · · + α_0 + · · · + α_r x^{−r}`. Alphabet is
exactly `A = F_p`, `p` prime. This is strictly stronger than "group
endomorphism": it is an endomorphism of the `F_p`-vector space `F_p^Z`.

The automaticity results:

> **Theorem 3.5.** Let Φ : F_p^Z → F_p^Z be a linear cellular automaton. If
> u ∈ F_p^Z is such that (u_m)_{m≥0} is p-automatic and u_m = 0 for all m ≤ −1,
> then the generating function of ST_Φ(u) is algebraic and so has a finite
> [p,p]-kernel.

> **Theorem 3.14.** … then ST_Φ(u) is [−p,p]-automatic.

> **Corollary 3.15.** … If u ∈ F_p^Z is (−p)-automatic, then ST_Φ(u) is
> [−p,p]-automatic.

The measure results:

> **Theorem 6.11.** Let Φ be a linear cellular automaton, and let U ∈ F_p^{Z×Z}
> be a [−p,−p]-automatic spacetime diagram for Φ. Then there exists a simplex of
> (σ₁,σ₂)-invariant measures generated by the relevant Perron vectors of the
> incidence matrices of the four substitutions defining U.

**(b) Consumption — one arrow, and it is the first.** From the proof of Thm. 3.5
(arXiv pp. 8-9), verbatim:

> "The n-th row of ST_Φ(u) is the sequence whose generating function is the
> Laurent series φ(x)^n f_u(x). […] F_U(x,y) = Σ_{n=0}^∞ φ(x)^n f_u(x) y^n =
> f_u(x) / (1 − φ(x)y)."

`row_n = φ(x)^n f_u(x)`, i.e. the closed-form geometric series
`F_U = f_u/(1−φy)`, is the single point where linearity is spent: `Φ` must act as
multiplication by a fixed Laurent polynomial on generating functions. Linearity
is spent twice more, in Cor. 3.15 ("Since Φ is linear, ST_Φ(u) is the termwise
sum of these two spacetime diagrams", p. 14) and in Prop. 5.3 (periodicity
reduces to `(φ(x)^{−j} − x^i) f_u(x) = 0`).

Everything downstream is **rule-agnostic**: Christol (**Thm. 3.1**), algebraic ⟺
finite `[p,p]`-kernel (**Thm. 3.4**), Cobham (**Thm. 5.6(2)**: "[p,p]-automatic
if and only if it is the image, under a coding, of a fixed point of a
substitution θ : A → A^{p×p}"), and the Perron-vector construction of measures
(**Thm. 6.11**).

**(c) Rule 30, and what the minimal weakening would have to be.** Rule 30 is not
`F_p`-linear, so `row_n = φ^n f_u` has no analogue and the whole chain is
unstarted. **The minimal weakening is not "relax linearity a bit":** Nesme's
extension (arXiv:2207.13062v5, DMTCS 28:3 #8 (2026); DOI unverified, Crossref
404 on `10.46298/dmtcs.9846`) pushes `F_p`-linearity down to a commutative
monoid endomorphism —

> **Theorem 3.** Let p be a prime number and Σ a finite commutative monoid such
> that π(Σ) ⊆ {p}. Let F : Σ^Z → Σ^Z be a cellular automaton that is also an
> endomorphism of Σ^Z. If the initial configuration c : Z → Σ is p-automatic,
> then so is its spacetime diagram.

— and then proves the weakening **cannot be pushed further**:

> (p. 2) "One could ask whether we really do need an identity element in Σ. What
> if (Σ,·) is just a commutative semigroup? In that case, the algebraic structure
> is too lax. […] This shows that the dynamics of any arbitrary cellular
> automaton can be replicated by a cellular automaton that is also a commutative
> semigroup endomorphism. It follows that, to obtain nontrivial results about the
> dynamics of a cellular automaton that is an endomorphism of some algebraic
> structure, this structure has to be more rigid than a mere commutative
> semigroup."

That is a **documented impossibility for the weakening strategy**: at semigroup
level the class already contains *every* CA, so no automaticity theorem can hold
there. Rule 30 is not a monoid endomorphism (§2). **Any nonlinear analog must
abandon the algebraic route, not relax it.**

**(T) Partly yes — this is the only entry in the right category.** Thm. 6.11's
measures are `(σ₁,σ₂)`-invariant measures on a two-dimensional subshift `X_U`
built from a spacetime diagram. That is the same kind of object as R8's `Y`.
But note it produces a **simplex**, not a unique measure: even in the linear
case, R-Y does not deliver a single number.

**(E) Wrong side — and this is the finding that matters most.** The paper
contains **no** statement about the frequency of a symbol in one specified
column, and no unique-ergodicity or generic-point statement for a named orbit.
The closest is a methodological remark (arXiv p. 24, journal p. 1714):

> "In Theorem 6.11 we identify (σ,Φ)-invariant measures as belonging to simplices
> whose extreme points are ergodic measures supported on codings of substitutional
> shifts. This statement implicitly contains another method by which to determine
> whether μ is trivial, as there exist algorithms to compute the frequency of a
> word for such a measure."

That is frequency **with respect to a measure**, not along one column of one
diagram. **So the R-Y program does not cross obstruction E even in the linear
case it was written for.** R8's hope — that orbit-closure classification converts
a.e. statements into everywhere statements — is not something R-Y demonstrates.

**(F) mode (ii): consistent, and instructively so.** Rule 90 is `F_2`-linear with
a 2-automatic lone-seed initial condition, so Thms. 3.5/3.14 apply and return the
Sierpinski substitution structure; Thm. 6.11 then returns a simplex of
`(σ₁,σ₂)`-invariant measures, **which contains the degenerate measure with centre
density 0**. The machinery therefore gives the correct Rule 90 answer rather than
a false claim — but it gives it *as a simplex member*, not as a dichotomy. The
nontriviality half never fires, because the lone seed is eventually periodic
(§0 Correction 1). This is a consistency check only; per §1 constraint 2 it is
strictly weaker than passing mode (i), and it is **not** evidence for anything
about Rule 30.

## 4. What every entry has in common, stated once

Two independent walls, and each entry hits at least one.

**Wall 1 — degenerate measures are excluded by hypothesis, never by conclusion.**
Sablik states it outright on p. 1: "every uniform measure supported on a
(F,σ)-periodic orbit is (F,σ)-invariant. We want to obtain additional conditions
which allow us to characterize the uniform Bernoulli measure." Pivato does it with
`h_μ(Φ) > 0` and total σ-ergodicity; Tal with positive entropy in his Open
Problem. Every one of these hypotheses is a property of the measure. For R8, the
measures in question are the weak-* limit points of empirical averages along the
lone-seed column, and **verifying positive entropy or ergodicity for those is at
least as hard as knowing the column** — the circularity `PATH.md` R8 already names
as its honest risk. *This is the exact reason a rigidity theorem, even a perfect
nonlinear one, would land on the wrong side of obstruction E.*

**Wall 2 — Pivato's own warning about invariant measures as evidence.** Survey
**Remark 2D.7**, verbatim:

> "The Universal Representation Corollary implies that studying the measurable
> dynamics of the CA Φ with respect to some arbitrary Φ-invariant measure ν will
> generally tell us nothing whatsoever about Φ."

Any future R8 work that produces *an* invariant measure on `Y` and reads a
density off it must answer this remark first.

## 5. The named open problem

R8 needs the Rowland-Yassawi chain to run for a nonlinear rule. The chain,
verified above, is:

```
Φ is F_p-linear
  --(1)--> row_n = φ(x)^n f_u(x), so F_U = f_u/(1 - φ(x)y) is ALGEBRAIC   [Thm 3.5]
  --(2)--> F_U has a finite [p,p]-kernel                                   [Thm 3.4, Christol/Salon]
  --(3)--> ST_Φ(u) is [-p,p]-automatic                                     [Thm 3.14, Cor 3.15]
  --(4)--> ST is a coding of a fixed point of a p x p substitution         [Thm 5.6(2), Cobham]
  --(5)--> invariant measures = simplex from Perron vectors of incidence   [Thm 6.11]
```

**Arrows (2) through (5) are rule-agnostic and already published.** Arrow (1) is
the only one that consumes linearity, and it consumes it totally: it is the
statement that `Φ` acts as multiplication by a fixed Laurent polynomial.

> ### OPEN PROBLEM RY-NL (the renormalization certificate).
> Exhibit a **non-algebraic mechanism** producing finite-index self-similarity in
> the lone-seed spacetime diagram of a left-permutive, non-right-permutive,
> non-endomorphic cellular automaton. Concretely: a finite kernel under some
> decimation, or an S-adic / Bratteli-Vershik presentation of the diagram (or of
> the orbit closure `Y`) with a controlled sequence of substitution matrices,
> derived from the local rule rather than from an algebraic identity.
>
> **This is unposed in print.** No published mechanism produces one. Nesme
> (arXiv:2207.13062v5 p. 2) proves the algebraic route cannot be relaxed to reach
> it: at commutative-semigroup level the hypothesis class already contains every
> CA, so no automaticity theorem can hold there. Any solution must abandon the
> algebraic route rather than weaken it.
>
> **RY-NL is insufficient by construction, not merely incomplete.** Solving
> arrow (1) for a nonlinear rule delivers arrows (2)-(5) and therefore delivers
> **a simplex** (Thm. 6.11) and nothing more. The results that would exclude the
> degenerate members of that simplex — Cor. 6.3, Thms. 6.4/6.9 — all require an
> initial condition that is **not eventually periodic**, and a lone seed never
> is. So the shortfall is not an artifact of nonlinearity that a better arrow (1)
> could repair; it is forced by the object R8 studies. §0 Correction 1 exhibits
> the shortfall concretely: for Rule 90 the linear chain runs to completion and
> the resulting simplex contains the degenerate centre-density-0 measure, which
> is exactly why the R8 claim is false there. **A solver of RY-NL should expect
> the same outcome for Rule 30 and must plan for RY-NL-b from the start.**

RY-NL-b below is therefore the named consequence of RY-NL, not a sibling gap.
RY-NL-c is genuinely independent.

> ### RY-NL-b (the value, not the existence) — consequence of RY-NL.
> Even granting RY-NL in its strongest form — `(Y, vertical shift)` uniquely
> ergodic — what follows is that the limiting centre density **exists**, not that
> it **equals 1/2**. Rowland-Yassawi Thm. 6.11 produces a **simplex** of measures
> even in the linear case, and §0 Correction 1 shows a degenerate member surviving
> in it for a lone seed. Identifying the limit measure, or excluding the
> degenerate members without an aperiodicity hypothesis nobody can supply for a
> lone seed, is the second problem, and the R-Y analog does not touch it. R8's
> stated target ("every vertical-shift-invariant measure on `Y` assigns the centre
> cell density 1/2") is strictly stronger than anything the R-Y chain delivers.

> ### SEPARATE GAP RY-NL-c (the object).
> Every rigidity theorem in §3 classifies `(σ, F)`-invariant measures on `A^Z`.
> R8's `Y` carries only the vertical shift and is the closure of one orbit.
> Whether `Y` is horizontally shift-invariant, and whether the two measure classes
> are comparable at all, is not settled by any source read here. The sibling arm's
> 2D patch-recurrence measurement bears on this; this arm does not resolve it.

### 5.1 The cheapest sub-problem RY-NL produces

RY-NL for the **column** rather than the diagram is already a named repo target:

> **Is A051023 `k`-automatic for some `k`?**

If yes, P2 reduces to a finite computation, because frequencies of automatic
sequences exist and are computable. This is **not** asserted to be false here.
`ARM6-binary-kernel.md` explicitly states its own limit — "This arm is not a
proof of non-automaticity" — and register row 46 records the reduction
`conjunction of all S(n) ⟺ non-2-automaticity of A051023` (Christol), stalled on

finite data buys a kernel lower bound and nothing more.

Keep **column**-automaticity and **diagram**-automaticity distinct. R-Y's
theorems are about the *diagram*; ARM6 and row 46 are about the *column*. They
are related but not the same statement, and neither implies the other without
work that has not been done here.
