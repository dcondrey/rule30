## 1. Bridy, Proposition 2.13 — verbatim, p. 7

> **Proposition 2.13.** *Let* `y = sum_{n=0}^{infinity} a(n)x^n` *in*
> `F_q[[x]]`. *Assume* **a** *is* `q`*-automatic and* `N_q(a) = m`. *Then* `y`
> *is algebraic,* `deg(y) <= q^m - 1`, *and* `h(y) <= mq^{m+1}`.

Definitions, Bridy p. 1, verbatim:

> "Let `N_q(a)` denote the number of states in a minimal `q`-automaton that
> generates **a** in the reverse-reading convention (this will be defined
> precisely in Section 2). If `y` is algebraic over `k(x)`, let the *degree*
> `deg(y)` be the usual field degree `[k(x)[y] : k(x)]` and the *height* `h(y)`
> be the minimal `x`-degree of a bivariate polynomial `f(x,T)` in `k[x,T]` such
> that `f(x,y) = 0`."

And p. 7, in the run-up to Prop. 2.13:

> "If **a** is a `q`-automatic sequence, then by Eilenberg's Theorem `S_q(y)` is
> finite and `|S_q(y)| = N_q(a)`."

So `N_q(a)` = reverse-reading state complexity = `|q`-kernel`|`. This is the
same identification A–Y make explicitly at their eq. (1) (§2 below).

**The proof's last sentence is the part that bears on row 46, and it is not the
proposition. p. 7, verbatim:**

> "Working through the chain of linear dependences shows that each `y_i^{q^k}`
> can be written as a linear combination of `{y_1^{q^{m+1}}, ..., y_m^{q^{m+1}}}`
> with polynomial coefficients of degree at most `q^{m+1}`. A standard argument
> in linear algebra shows that there is a vanishing linear combination of
> `{y_i, y_i^q, ..., y_i^{q^m}}` with polynomial coefficients of degree at most
> `mq^{m+1}`."

**This sentence, not the statement of Prop. 2.13, is what must be cited against
row 46.** Prop. 2.13 as stated bounds `h(y)`, a *minimal-polynomial* quantity.
The *Ore-relation* height bound — a vanishing `F_q(x)`-combination of
`{y, y^q, ..., y^{q^m}}`, order `<= m`, coefficient degrees `<= mq^{m+1}` — lives
only in the proof. In characteristic `p`, `y^{q^i} = y(x^{q^i})`, so that
combination is literally row 46's Ore relation
`P_{-1} + sum_{i=0}^{n} P_i F(x^{2^i}) = 0`. The Frobenius rewriting is standard
but is this document's phrasing, not Bridy's; he never writes "Mahler".

Note also the inequality `h(y) <= Ore-height(y)`: an Ore relation
`Q_{-1} + sum_i Q_i y^{q^i} = 0` *is* an annihilating bivariate polynomial
`f(x,T) = Q_{-1}(x) + sum_i Q_i(x) T^{q^i}` whose `x`-degree is the Ore height.
So a lower bound on `h` is a lower bound on Ore height. §4 uses this.

## 2. Adamczewski–Yassawi, "A note on Christol's theorem" — verbatim, p. 2

> "We are interested here in the interplay between these two notions of
> complexity. If a sequence **a** is generated by a `q`-automaton with at most
> `m` states, it is not difficult to show that the associated power series `f`
> has degree at most `q^m − 1` and height at most `mq^{m+1}` (see, for instance,
> [Bri17, Proposition 2.13]). Furthermore, these bounds cannot be significantly
> improved in general."

a17 §7.2's quotation of this sentence is **exact**. The bound they restate is
Bridy's, with the explicit citation to Prop. 2.13, so it is the same bound and
not a different one. Their `m` is the state count, matching Bridy: their p. 2
eq. (1) states `comp_q(a) = |ker_q(a)|` citing [AS03, Cor. 4.1.9 and Thm 6.6.2],
and their p. 1 defines `comp_q(a)` as "the number of states in a minimal finite
automaton generating **a** in *reverse* reading". Their `height` definition
(p. 1) is identical to Bridy's: "the height of `f` is the minimal degree (in `x`)
of a nonzero polynomial `P(x,y)` in `F_q[x,y]` such that `P(x, f(x)) = 0`."

**Do not lean on this sentence for the refutation.** It carries no citation and
is a stronger assertion than Bridy makes about his own bounds. Bridy, p. 8,
immediately after Prop. 2.13, verbatim:

> "It is easy to construct infinite families of power series for which degree and
> height grow exponentially in `N_q(a)`, which we do in Examples 2.14 and 2.15.
> **It is not clear whether the bounds of Proposition 2.13 are sharper than these
> families indicate.**"

Side by side these say different things. A–Y assert non-improvability; Bridy
declines to. The two are consistent — "cannot be significantly improved" and "it
is not clear whether the bounds are sharper" agree that *exponential* is the
right order and disagree only on the polynomial factor — but the **construction**
is the primary evidence and A–Y's sentence is a secondary, uncited restatement.
The refutation in §4 rests entirely on the construction.

## 3. Verdict question 1 — do Bridy and row 46 bound the same object?

**Three distinct objects are in play, and a17's flag conflated two of them.**

**(a) a6's Theorem N / `G_n`.** *Not* the object of any `m·2^{2m}` bound. a6's
Theorem N proves `G_n` has "an Ore relation of order exactly `n`, **at height
1**". The task brief's framing — "row 46 proves Theorem N: the Ore-relation
ladder for `G_n` has height `<= m·2^{2m}`" — is a misreading of a6. `G_n` has
height 1. `G_n` is a witness family for ladder strictness, not an instance of the
height bound. **Different objects; on this pairing there is no issue at all.**

**(b) a6 §4's general bound `height <= m·2^{2m}`.** This *is* the same object as
Bridy's proof sentence: the coefficient degrees of an Ore relation for the power
series of a 2-automatic sequence. Same notion of height (max `deg P_i`), same
notion of order, same relation shape. **Same object — with one variable
mismatch that must not be glossed.**

**The variable mismatch.** a6 §4's `m` is "the GF(2) dimension of the span of its
2-kernel". Bridy's `m = N_q(a)` is the *cardinality* `|ker_q(a)|`, equivalently
the reverse-reading state count `k`. These are different quantities. Writing
`m_span` for a6's and `k` for Bridy's:

- `m_span <= k` (the kernel is `k` sequences, its span has dimension at most `k`);
- `k <= 2^{m_span}` (every kernel element lies in the span, which has `2^{m_span}`
  elements).

So `m·2^{m+1}` and `m·2^{2m}` are **not comparable as written**. State the
comparison in one variable, and it must be the state-count variable `k`, because
that is the variable Lemma L is stated in:

| bound, expressed in state count `k` | value |
|---|---|
| Bridy (proof of Prop. 2.13), `q=2` | `k · 2^{k+1}` |
| a6 §4, using `m_span <= k` | `<= k · 2^{2k}` |

**In the state-count variable Bridy is strictly better**, by a factor `2^{k-1}`.
In the span-dimension variable a6's is better and Bridy's does not apply directly
(substituting `k <= 2^{m_span}` into Bridy gives `2^{m_span}·2^{2^{m_span}+1}`, a
tower). The honest statement is the table above, in `k`.

## 4. Verdict question 2 — not tight, or false?

**a6 §4's proved bound `m·2^{2m}` is NOT FALSE. It is a valid upper bound that is
not tight.** `k·2^{k+1} < k·2^{2k}` for every `k >= 2`, with equality at `k = 1`
(both `4`). Two valid upper bounds on the same quantity, one smaller: the larger
is simply not best possible. Nothing in a6's derivation is wrong; §4 already
diagnoses its own slack correctly ("The exponential in the proved bound comes
**entirely** from degree growth in that iterated Frobenius product"). No
correction to a6 §4's mathematics is required. The only edit warranted is a
citation: Bridy's proof gives a better constant for free.

**Lemma L, however, is a different statement, and it is FALSE.** `PATH.md` row 46
states it verbatim:

> "LEMMA L. If `a` is 2-automatic with `k` states, then `F` admits an Ore
> relation of order `<= poly(log k)` and height `<= poly(k)`"

Both halves fail, by **Bridy Example 2.14, p. 8, verbatim**:

> "**Example 2.14.** Let `y = x^n` and let **a** be the sequence with a 1 in the
> `n`th position and 0 in every other position. We have
> `N_q(a) = ceil(log_q(n)) + 1`, because a `q`-DFAO generating **a** needs
> `ceil(log_q(n))` states to recognize the base-`q` expansion of `n` and one
> additional 'trap state' that outputs zero on any input that deviates from this
> expansion. **So `h(y)` grows exponentially in the number of states required.**"

**Height half, refuted.** `y = x^n` has `h(y) = n` exactly: the minimal
polynomial of `x^n` over `F_q(x)` is `T − x^n`, so every annihilator in
`F_q[x,T]` is a multiple of it and has `x`-degree `>= n`. By the inequality
`h(y) <= Ore-height(y)` from §1, the minimal Ore height of `x^n` is `>= n`.

State the consequence in the **existential** form, which is the one that is
exactly true. `k = N_2(a) = ceil(log_2 n) + 1` inverts to
`2^{k-2} < n <= 2^{k-1}`, so the universally valid bound is only
`Ore-height > 2^{k-2}`. Taking `n = 2^{k-1}` gives `N_2(a) = k` exactly and
minimal Ore height exactly `2^{k-1}`:

> **For every `k >= 2` there is a 2-automatic sequence with exactly `k` states
> whose minimal Ore height is exactly `2^{k-1}`** (the indicator of
> `{2^{k-1}}`, i.e. `y = x^{2^{k-1}}`).

So `height <= poly(k)` is false, by an explicit two-line example. This answers a6
§4's own sharpened question —

> "Does there exist a `k`-state 2-automatic sequence whose minimal Ore height is
> `2^(Theta(k))`, or is the true height bound `poly(k)`?"

— **YES, and it is in print since 2016.** The finite search a6 §4 proposed at
each `k` does not need to be run. (a6 §4 says that search "is the correct next
target for row 46 and is left uncomputed here on purpose". It should be struck,
not run.)

**Order half, also refuted, by Example 2.15 (see §5).** `G_n = ` Bridy's
Ex. 2.15 series has `N_q(a) = m + 2` and `deg(y) = q^{N_q(a)-2} = q^m` (Bridy's
own statement, quoted in §5). An Ore relation of order `r` yields an annihilating
polynomial of `T`-degree `q^r`, so `deg(y) = q^m` forces minimal Ore order
exactly `m = k − 2`, **linear in `k`, not `poly(log k)`.** (The step from
`deg(y) = q^m` to minimal order `m` is this document's inference; Bridy states
the degree, not the order. It is the same conclusion a6's own separation lemma
reaches independently, so it is doubly supported.)

**Internal inconsistency, flagged.** `PATH.md` row 46 states Lemma L's order half
as `order <= poly(log k)`. a6 §4 says "the order half, order `<= m`, is already
proved" and a6's title line repeats this. `m_span` is not `poly(log k)` — it can
be as large as `k`. So a6 is claiming as proved a *weaker* statement than the one
`PATH.md` records as the obligation. Whichever is intended, `poly(log k)` is now
refuted, and the two documents should be made to agree.

## 5. a6's Theorem N witness family is Bridy Example 2.15

**Bridy, Example 2.15, p. 8, verbatim (opening):**

> "**Example 2.15.** The degree bound of Proposition 2.13 is nearly sharp for
> those degrees that are powers of `q`. We argue that the unique solution in
> `F_q[[x]]` to the Artin-Schreier equation
>
>     y^{q^m} − y = x,
>
> which is
>
>     y = x + x^{q^m} + x^{q^{2m}} + x^{q^{3m}} + ... ,
>
> satisfies `N_q(a) = m + 2`, so `deg(y) = q^{N_q(a)−2}`."

Set `q = 2`, `m = n`:

    y = x + x^{2^n} + x^{2^{2n}} + x^{2^{3n}} + ...  =  sum_{j>=0} x^{2^{nj}}  =  G_n(x).

**Identical series.** And a6's relation, quoted from a6 §1,

    x + G_n(x) + G_n(x^{2^n}) = 0,

is exactly `y^{2^n} − y = x` rearranged over `F_2` (in characteristic 2,
`y^{2^n} = y(x^{2^n})` and `−1 = +1`). It is the Artin–Schreier equation.

Consequences for a6, all of them credit corrections rather than invalidations:

1. **Theorem N's witness family is prior art**, published 2016 in the paper this
   document is verifying. Bridy's stated purpose for it is narrower than a6's:
   his opening sentence is "The **degree** bound of Proposition 2.13 is nearly
   sharp for those degrees that are powers of `q`" — Ex. 2.15 is his
   *degree*-sharpness example, and Ex. 2.14 is the *height*-vs-states one. The
   order/height reading used here is derived from his stated `deg(y)`, not his
   framing.
2. **Theorem N's lower bound is a rediscovery.** a6's "separation lemma" — the
   disjoint-Frobenius-support argument proving minimal order exactly `n` — is
   re-derivable in one line from Bridy's stated `deg(y) = q^m`. a6's proof is
   correct and self-contained; it is not new.
3. **Bridy hands a6 the state count for free:** `N_2(G_n) = n + 2`. a6 never
   computes it. This is what makes `G_n` a counterexample to Lemma L's order
   half (§4) rather than merely a strictness witness.
4. a6's *use* of the family — extending ladder strictness from rung 0 to every
   level `n`, in the ladder's own `S(n)` coordinates — is not in Bridy, and
   Theorem N's conclusion in that form stands. Only the "explicit power series"
   and "proved witness family" novelty framing needs the citation attached.

## 6. Verdict question 3 — what A–Y's non-improvability claim implies for `G_n`

**Nothing specific to `G_n`, and it is not needed.**

A–Y's sentence is a statement about the *general class*: no substantially better
bound holds for all `k`-state 2-automatic sequences. It is an assertion about the
worst case, so it cannot say anything about any particular sequence's minimal
Ore height. For `G_n` specifically nothing needs to be inferred from it, because
`G_n`'s minimal Ore relation is *known exactly*: order `n`, height `1` (a6
Theorem N, upper bound machine-verified; lower bound by a6's separation lemma and
independently by Bridy's `deg(y) = q^m`). `G_n` is a sequence with `n+2` states
whose Ore height is `1` — it is a witness for the *order* lower bound, not the
height one. The height counterexample is the different, simpler series `x^n`
(Ex. 2.14).

So: A–Y's claim, if right, means no `poly(k)` height bound exists for the general
class. Bridy Ex. 2.14 proves that outright without needing A–Y. Neither bears on
Rule 30's A051023 in any way — Lemma L was always a statement about the class of
automatic sequences, never about Rule 30 (a6 §4 says this correctly:
"This is a question about the class of automatic sequences ... **not** a question
about Rule 30 or about any prefix of A051023").

## 7. The usable payoff for row 46: an explicit exponential data-cost wall

The negative is not the whole result. Bridy's bound is *effective*, and combined
with a6's Theorem U it converts row 46's route into a quantified wall.

If `a` is 2-automatic with `k` states then, by the proof of Prop. 2.13, `F`
admits an Ore relation of order `<= k` and height `<= k·2^{k+1}`. Contrapositive:
certifying `S(k, k·2^{k+1})` — no Ore relation at order `k` up to that height —
**excludes every 2-automatic sequence with at most `k` states.** a6's Theorem U
prices that: `N >= (n+2)(d+1)` terms of `a` are needed, so

    N(k)  =  (k + 2) · (k·2^{k+1} + 1).

| `k` (states excluded) | terms `N(k)` required |
|---:|---:|
| 3 | 245 |
| 5 | 2,247 |
| 6 | 6,152 |
| **7** | **16,137** |
| 8 | 40,970 |
| 12 | 1,376,270 |
| 16 | 37,748,754 |
| **20** | **922,746,902** |

**At the register's `N = 32000`, row 46's ladder can exclude 2-automaticity only
up to `k = 7` states. At `10^9` bits — Wen's billion-bit computation, the largest
centre column ever produced — it reaches only `k = 20`.** Non-2-automaticity of
A051023 requires all `k`, so the gap is not large, it is unbounded, and it grows
exponentially in exactly the parameter being swept.

This is a *sharper* and *citable* form of obstruction H (finite-prefix data
bounds a complexity function and never establishes the infinite statement): the
route's cost is now `Theta(k^2 2^k)` terms per state-count band, from a published
bound rather than from a conjecture. It also explains why a6's certificates
(order `<= 12`, heights to 570) sit so comfortably inside the reachable region —
they are nowhere near the height an automaticity exclusion would need.

**One convention gap, and it runs the safe way.** Bridy's relation is
**homogeneous** — `sum_j c_j(x) y^{q^j} = 0`, no free term — while row 46's form
carries `P_{-1}`. Homogeneous relations are the `P_{-1} = 0` subset of row 46's
space, so a6's certificates rule out a *strictly larger* space than Bridy's bound
inhabits, and §7's exclusion logic is sound as stated. The two conventions differ
for a reason worth keeping: it is the `P_{-1}` term that makes `S(0) <=> P1`,
since an order-0 *homogeneous* relation `P_0 F = 0` says `F = 0`, not that `F` is
rational.

Two further caveats on the table. The direction used is the sufficient one (Bridy gives
an *upper* bound on height, so exceeding it excludes); a smaller `d` may suffice
in practice, this is a ceiling on what is *needed*, not on what would work.
And Theorem U's counting bound is a necessary condition on `N`, not a guarantee
that the rank computation is feasible at those sizes.
