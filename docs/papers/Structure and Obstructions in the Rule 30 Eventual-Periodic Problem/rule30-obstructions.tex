\begin{abstract}
Wolfram's Prize Problem~1 asks whether the center column of Rule~30's
lone-seed orbit is non-periodic. We leave Problems~1, 2 and 3 open. Instead,
we map the provable/unprovable boundary. First, we fully characterize eventual
period-one for all nonzero finite configurations: the two constant-trace
fibers avoid finitely supported configurations off-origin, and the sharp
constant-prefix horizon is $w+2$. Second, we delimit eventual period-two: a
mechanized $\omega$-automaton ladder --- proved uniform in right depth, left
depth, and diagonal bound --- cannot close the case; an exhaustive
forced-continuation census to $n=24$ and $n=30$ by a verified fast method
shows exclusion hinges on a bounded set of extremal forcing states. Third, we
catalog ninety-four failed attempts in nine obstruction classes, unified by
one fact: Rule~30's nonlinearity is its only difference from additive
Rule~90, whose center column is eventually periodic, so every
nonlinearity-free proof must fail. A route-by-route compendium and
computational archive accompany the paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================

Rule~30 is the binary cellular automaton on $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z}$ with local rule
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule30}
 F(x)_i=x_{i-1}\oplus(x_i\lor x_{i+1}),
\end{equation}
left permutive: fixing $x_i,x_{i+1}$, the value $F(x)_i$ is a bijection of
$x_{i-1}$.

Wolfram's Rule~30 Prize poses three problems about the center column of the
lone-seed orbit \cite{wolfram}: whether it is always non-periodic
(Problem~1), whether each color occurs with equal asymptotic frequency
(Problem~2), and whether computing its $n$th cell requires $\Omega(n)$
effort (Problem~3). The column is verified non-periodic only through $10^9$
steps, and no finite check can settle Problem~1 --- a limit we return to as
obstruction~H (Section~\ref{sec:register}). We address Problem~1 throughout,
Problem~2 through the period-two thread, and Problem~3 only in the register.

The finite-configuration version of Problem~1 is open \cite{kopra-thesis}: at
most one column can be eventually periodic \cite[Thm.~2b]{jen1986}\cite[Prop.~3]{jen},
and the width-two column is never eventually periodic, though the technique
does not extend to width one \cite{kopra}.

This paper is organized around one fact that we make load-bearing throughout.
Rule~90, $F(x)_i = x_{i-1}\oplus x_{i+1}$, is also left permutive, yet its
lone-seed center column \emph{is} eventually periodic (identically zero off the
initial step). Hence no argument that uses only left permutivity, additivity, or
any property Rule~90 shares can decide Problem~1: it would prove a false
statement about Rule~90. The single property Rule~30 has that Rule~90 lacks is
the \textsc{or} in \eqref{eq:rule30}, which latches. We call the requirement that
an argument depend on this latch the \emph{Rule~90 filter}. Every positive
result below passes it; every failure catalogued in Part~IV either violates it
or hits one of eight further walls.

\paragraph{What is proved.} Part~I settles eventual period one
(Section~\ref{sec:periodone}): both constant-trace fibers are computed in closed
form, the finite configurations meet them only at the origin, and the horizon is
sharp. Part~III proves three limitation theorems for the period-two ladder
(Section~\ref{sec:ladder}). These are honest negative results: they say a
specific, natural, mechanized method cannot close period two, and why.

\paragraph{What is measured, and what is conjectured.} Part~II
(Section~\ref{sec:periodtwo}) reduces period-two exclusion to a finite-word
statement, carries the exhaustive census to $n=24$ (and a verified fast method to
$n=30$), and reports a structural phenomenon: near extinction the surviving
witnesses collapse onto $O(1)$ extremal forcing states sharing a long common
suffix and a single failure symbol. We state this as a conjecture with its
supporting data, never as a theorem.

\paragraph{What fails, and why.} Part~IV (Section~\ref{sec:register}) is the
register. Nine obstruction classes account for the ninety-four
routes attempted across this project. We give each class, the routes it kills,
the kill-reason type, and its Rule~90 status. The exhaustive per-route list is
the accompanying supplement.

\paragraph{Scope.} Part~V (Section~\ref{sec:scope}) states precisely what is and
is not reached, against the published state of the art, and lists the concrete
open leads. We claim no progress on the prize problems themselves.

\section{Part I: Finite configurations cannot generate a constant trace}
\label{sec:periodone}
% ============================================================================

This part is lifted, statement and proof, from a companion note carrying the
full derivation, machine-checked Lean and SMT certificates, and a
reproducibility appendix; only the two figures and the eight results are
reproduced here.

We write $c_t:=\Tr(x)_t=F^t(x)_0$, $R_j:=x_j$, $L_j:=x_{-j}$ ($j\ge0$), and let
$\F$ denote the finitely supported configurations. The trace sees only column
$0$, but under a left-permutive rule a discrepancy left of the origin
propagates rightward along a diagonal and must eventually reach it. The
classification rests on this one fact, which uses only locality and left
permutivity, not the specific form of \eqref{eq:rule30}.

\begin{lemma}[Triangular uniqueness]\label{lem:unique}
If two configurations agree at every coordinate $i\ge0$ and have the same
central trace, then they are equal.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $-n$ is their rightmost differing coordinate, locality preserves agreement
to its right through time $n$. Left permutivity preserves the discrepancy
along $(-n,0),(-n+1,1),\dots,(0,n)$, contradicting equal traces. In its finite
form, if the rows agree on $i\ge0$ and their traces agree for $0\le t\le r$,
the same induction forces agreement at $-r,-r+1,\dots,-1$.
\end{proof}

Uniqueness alone does not say what the forced left half looks like. The next
result computes it: the rows with zero trace form one invariant class
$\mathcal C_m$ for each position $m$ of the first right-hand one, each class is
carried to its predecessor by $F$, and the left half is a prefix-OR of the
right. Figure~\ref{fig:panels}(a) is a member of $\mathcal C_3$.

\begin{theorem}[Zero-trace fiber]\label{thm:fiber}
For a prescribed right half with $R_0=0$, the zero-trace-compatible left half is
unique. If the positive half is nonzero and $m=\min\{j\ge1:R_j=1\}$, the forced
left half is $L_j=0\ (j<m)$, $L_m=1$, $L_j=j\bmod2\ (j>m)$, with $R_j$ for
$j>m$ arbitrary. If instead the positive half is zero, the unique row is zero.
Equivalently, in prefix-OR form,
\begin{align}
 L_{2k+1}&=\bigvee_{j=1}^{2k+1}R_j &&(k\ge0),\\[-2pt]
 L_{2k}&=R_{2k}\land\neg\!\bigvee_{j=1}^{2k-1}R_j &&(k\ge1).
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $m\ge1$, let $\mathcal C_m$ consist of rows satisfying these conditions.
One Rule~30 step gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:updates}
\begin{aligned}
 x'_0&=L_1\oplus R_1, &
 R'_j&=R_{j-1}\oplus(R_j\lor R_{j+1}), &
 L'_j&=L_{j+1}\oplus(L_j\lor L_{j-1}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Suppose $m>1$. These identities give $x'_0=0$, $R'_j=L'_j=0\ (j<m-1)$,
$R'_{m-1}=L'_{m-1}=1$, $L'_m=m\bmod2$. For $j>m$, including $j=m+1$ because
$L_m=1$, the OR in the last update is one, so $L'_j=(1-(j\bmod2))\oplus1=j\bmod2$.
Thus $F(\mathcal C_m)\subseteq\mathcal C_{m-1}$. For $m=1$, the alternating left
half is fixed, $x'_0=1\oplus1=0$, and $R'_1=0\oplus(1\lor R_2)=1$ independently
of the right tail; hence $F(\mathcal C_1)\subseteq\mathcal C_1$. Every
$\mathcal C_m$ therefore realizes the zero trace. A nonzero right half selects a
realization by its first one, and Lemma~\ref{lem:unique} makes it unique; a
zero right half forces a zero left half by the same lemma against the zero row.
\end{proof}

The other constant trace has a simpler fiber: one row per right half, and the
left half no longer depends on which right half it is.

\begin{theorem}[All-one fiber]\label{thm:onefiber}
For a prescribed right half with $R_0=1$, the left half compatible with an
identically-one trace is unique and independent of the right half:
$L_j=1\iff j$ is positive and even.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $L_0:=x_0=R_0=1$, so the third identity of \eqref{eq:updates},
$L'_j=L_{j+1}\oplus(L_j\lor L_{j-1})$, applies at every $j\ge1$. Under the
checkerboard pattern it gives $0\oplus(1\lor0)=1$ for even $j$ and
$1\oplus(0\lor1)=0$ for odd $j$, so the left half is fixed by $F$ whatever the
right half is. At the origin, $F(x)_0=L_1\oplus(L_0\lor R_1)=0\oplus1=1$
independently of $R_1$, so the trace is identically one and $L_0=1$ is restored
at every step. Lemma~\ref{lem:unique} makes the left half unique.
\end{proof}

Every nonzero row in Theorem~\ref{thm:fiber} carries ones arbitrarily far to
the left, so none of them is finitely supported. That is the whole of the next
statement, once one checks that a finite orbit cannot become the zero row.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:finite}
$\Tr^{-1}(0^{\mathbb N})\cap\F=\{0\}$. Moreover, no column of the orbit of a
nonzero $x\in\F$ is eventually zero.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every nonzero row in Theorem~\ref{thm:fiber} has ones at all sufficiently large
odd left depths. If a column of a finite orbit were zero from time $T$ onward,
translation would make $F^T(x)$ a finite zero-trace row. It is nonzero: if
$[a,b]$ is the support interval of a nonzero finite row, its next row has ones
at $a-1$ and $b+1$ because Rule~30 maps $001$ and $100$ to one.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:finite} is not a consequence of left permutivity alone:
Rule~90 is also left permutive, yet the finite row with ones at $\pm1$ keeps a
zero center forever (Figure~\ref{fig:panels}(b,c)). The Rule-30-specific step
is the OR latch $R'_1=1\lor R_2=1$ in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fiber},
which has no analogue in an additive rule.

The same latch upgrades the corollary from one constant to both, with no
further machinery. Theorem~\ref{thm:onefiber} proves the second half again and
independently, since the checkerboard pattern has ones at every positive even
depth; the argument below is shorter and is the one to quote.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:constant}
No column of the orbit of a nonzero $x\in\F$ is eventually constant.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The eventually-zero case is Corollary~\ref{cor:finite}. Suppose instead some
column is eventually one; translating, take it to be column $0$, so $c_t=1$
for all $t\ge T$. Applying \eqref{eq:rule30} at the origin gives
$l_t=c_{t+1}\oplus(c_t\lor r_t)$, and $c_t=1$ collapses the disjunction, so
$l_t=1\oplus c_{t+1}$. For $t\ge T$ we also have $c_{t+1}=1$, hence $l_t=0$.
Column $-1$ is then zero from time $T$ onward, which Corollary~\ref{cor:finite}
forbids.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:finite} says a finite row's center cannot stay zero forever.
How long it can stay zero, as a function of the support radius, is exactly one
step past the light cone of the first odd depth beyond that radius. For $w\ge1$
let $\mathcal S_w=\{x\ne0:\supp(x)\subseteq[-w,w],x_0=0\}$, and for
$x\in\mathcal S_w$ let $H(x)=\max\{h:F^t(x)_0=0\text{ for }0\le t\le h\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp finite horizon]\label{thm:horizon}
Let $w\ge1$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \max_{x\in\mathcal S_w}H(x)=2\left\lceil\frac w2\right\rceil,\qquad
 \#\!\left\{x\in\mathcal S_w:H(x)=2\left\lceil\frac w2\right\rceil\right\}=2^w-1.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $q$ be the least odd integer greater than $w$, so $q-1=2\lceil w/2\rceil$.
If the positive right half is nonzero, a trace zero through time $q$, compared
with its zero-trace completion, would force by finite-prefix uniqueness and
the prefix-OR identity $x_{-q}=\bigvee_{j=1}^qR_j=1$, contradicting $q>w$. If
that half is zero, comparison with the zero row makes every nonzero left cell
visible by time $w$. Thus $H(x)\le q-1$. Conversely, choose any of the $2^w-1$
nonzero words $(R_1,\dots,R_w)$, install the prefix-OR pattern through left
depth $w$, and zero all other cells. This row agrees with its infinite
zero-trace completion through the light cone of time $q-1$; when $w$ is odd,
the extra depth $w+1$ is even and forced to zero. The missing odd cell at depth
$q$ creates a discrepancy at time $q$, and finite-prefix uniqueness excludes
further extremizers.
\end{proof}

The one-trace horizon is the same computation against the checkerboard fiber,
and the parities exchange roles: ones sit at even depths, so the binding depth
is the least \emph{even} one past the radius, and $R_0=1$ already makes the row
nonzero, which is why no extremizer is lost here.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp finite horizon, one]\label{thm:onehorizon}
Let $w\ge1$, let $\mathcal T_w=\{x:\supp(x)\subseteq[-w,w],x_0=1\}$, and for
$x\in\mathcal T_w$ let $H_1(x)=\max\{h:F^t(x)_0=1\text{ for }0\le t\le h\}$. Let
$q$ be the least even integer greater than $w$. Then
$\max_{x\in\mathcal T_w}H_1(x)=q-1$ and
$\#\{x\in\mathcal T_w:H_1(x)=q-1\}=2^w$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A trace one through time $q$, compared with the all-one completion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:onefiber}, would force by finite-prefix uniqueness
$x_{-q}=1$, contradicting $q>w$; thus $H_1(x)\le q-1$. Conversely, choose any of
the $2^w$ words $(R_1,\dots,R_w)$, install the checkerboard pattern through
left depth $w$, and zero all other cells. This row agrees with its all-one
completion through the light cone of time $q-1$; when $w$ is even, the extra
depth $w+1$ is odd and forced to zero. The missing even cell at depth $q$
creates a discrepancy at time $q$, and finite-prefix uniqueness excludes
further extremizers.
\end{proof}

Combining the two horizons, the sharp law over both constants is flat in the
parity that splits each one separately.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:bothhorizon}
Let $w\ge1$ and let $x\ne0$ have $\supp(x)\subseteq[-w,w]$. The longest
constant prefix of the central trace of $x$ has length at most $w+2$, and
exactly $2^w$ configurations attain length $w+2$ when $w$ is even, while
exactly $2^w-1$ attain it when $w$ is odd.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $x_0=0$ Theorem~\ref{thm:horizon} gives $2\lceil w/2\rceil$, which is $w$
for even $w$ and $w+1$ for odd $w$; for $x_0=1$ Theorem~\ref{thm:onehorizon}
gives $q-1$, which is $w+1$ for even $w$ and $w$ for odd $w$. Hence the maximum
horizon index is $w+1$, so the maximum prefix length is $w+2$. It is attained
in the one case when $w$ is even and in the zero case when $w$ is odd, with the
corresponding counts $2^w$ and $2^w-1$.
\end{proof}

The Rule-30-specific step is the \textsc{or} latch in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:fiber}: the same finite row under Rule~90 keeps a zero center
forever, so Corollary~\ref{cor:constant} is not a consequence of left
permutivity. This is the Rule~90 filter passing on a proven result, and the
template for reading every later obstruction.

\section{Part II: The period-two problem --- reduction, census, and a lead}
\label{sec:periodtwo}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{The reduction chain}

By Corollary~\ref{cor:constant} the constant traces are already excluded, so
the only remaining candidate counterexample is the alternating phase
$0101\ldots$; period-two exclusion reduces, through a chain of proved uniform
implications, to a single open separator. Writing (PT2) for ``no nonzero finite configuration has
$\Tr_0(y)=\Tr_0(F^2 y)$,'' the chain is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
 \gm(n)\ge1\ \forall n\ \Rightarrow\ H_r(n)=\emptyset\ \Rightarrow\
 \mathrm{RW}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \mathrm{DLP}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{SEP}\
 \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{(PT2)},
\end{equation}
in which every arrow is proved and only the terminal separator SEP is open.
Consequently a proof of $\gm(n)\ge1$ for all $n$ would yield the first
per-period exclusion theorem for the Rule~30 center column; the finite range is
already discharged (below).

\subsection{The forced-continuation census}

A source word $W\in\{1,2\}^n$ is extended by a deterministic forcing to a
continuation that must remain hard-core; $\mathrm{max\text{-}surv}(n)$ is the
most forced rows any $W$ survives and $\gm(n)=(n+2)-\mathrm{max\text{-}surv}(n)$.
The exhaustive range is pushed past brute force's practical $n\sim20$--$22$
ceiling by a dependency-edge dedup method: two source words reaching the same
forcing state are provably indistinguishable to every later step, so the
$2^n$ enumeration collapses to a walk over distinct states. The method is
verified bit-for-bit against the original recursion and cross-checked exactly
against brute force at every $n\le24$ with zero mismatches, a $\sim17\times$
speedup already at $n=18$.

\begin{proposition}[Census, exhaustive and verified]\label{prop:census}
$|H_r(n)|=0$ exhaustively for $n=1,\dots,24$, all residues and both constants;
the rotated-wedge SAT encoding is UNSAT to $n=30$; and $\gm(n)$, computed by a
fast dedup method cross-checked against brute force through $n=24$ with zero
mismatches, satisfies $\gm(n)\in[7,14]$ for $17\le n\le30$ with no monotone
trend.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
The apparent ``climbing'' trend in the even-$n$ subsequence is an artifact of
sampling only even $n$; the interleaved series shows no trend. One outlying
cell ($n=28$, $c=2$) is bracketed by its neighbors as a statistical spike, not
a signal, and is excluded below.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A structural lead: extinction is a needle, not a fog}

\begin{observation}[Terminal-class collapse; exhaustive $n\le18$]\label{obs:needle}
The words attaining $\mathrm{max\text{-}surv}(n)$ number between two and
eighteen, fall into one to four continuation classes, and within a class share a
common suffix of length $n-O(1)$, produce the identical forced continuation, and
fail on the identical symbol. The forcing's live state is identical across a
class (or differs only in the newest one or two cells). The phenomenon persists
under restriction to hard-core sources, where the extremal words are exactly the
clean periodic adversaries.
\end{observation}

The extremal continuations are concrete and recognizable: hard-core words
heavy in $2$s and nearly periodic, e.g.\ $22222122222$ and $2(221)^4$ ---
the forcing sits in a periodic orbit of the local map until a single phase
slip lands. Two supporting facts are proved: a suffix lemma (words agreeing on a suffix from
index $m$ and on the first $2m$ forced symbols share their continuation forever;
verified with zero exceptions) and that the sole Rule-30-specific ingredient of
the forcing is the collapse $\Phi(2,\cdot)=\Phi(3,\cdot)$ of two left-actions,
which is vacuous for Rule~90.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:needle}
For all $n$, $\gm(n)\ge1$; equivalently, no reachable forcing state at source
length $n$ survives $n+2$ forced rows. The extremal states, $O(1)$ per length,
are the whole content.
\end{conjecture}

The last clause of Observation~\ref{obs:needle} --- that under restriction to
hard-core sources the extremal words are the clean periodic adversaries --- was
the paper's one untried comparison, and it has since been tested and does not
survive. Enumerating every word attaining $\mathrm{max\text{-}surv}(n)$ for
$8\le n\le18$, with the per-tail maxima agreeing exactly with the project's
registered table, the genuinely short source periods $(1,3,4,5,6,7)$ occur only
through $n=11$; from $n=12$ the smallest period present in the extremal set
climbs with $n$, and a period of $n-1$ or $n-2$ on a length-$n$ word is not
periodicity but the tautology that a suffix matches a prefix. The single-defect
shape $2^a12^b$ accounts for four of the eleven extremal words at $n=8$, one at
$n=9$, one at $n=11$, and \emph{none} for $n\ge12$. The counting claim of the
observation is unaffected: the number attaining the maximum stays inside
$[2,18]$ throughout.

The consequence is that the extremal sources are not a bounded-period family,
so extinction on a finite list of periodic rays --- the shape that would have
carried a finite automaton or a one-dimensional recurrence --- is not available.
Extremal \emph{continuations} behave better, keeping small periods with only
occasional aperiodic cases, but that is a materially weaker statement and is not
what the finite-ray argument required. Conjecture~\ref{conj:needle} itself is
untouched; what is removed is the route to it that Observation~\ref{obs:needle}
appeared to offer.

\subsection{Why first-moment and spectral bounds cannot close it}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:spectral}
The hard-core transfer matrix has spectral radius exactly $\varphi=1.618\ldots$,
so the induced bound $\lambda<2$ holds vacuously at every width and cannot
witness extinction (which needs $\lambda<1$). The bound is tight for $k<k_\star$
and off by a factor $\approx300$ at extinction, so no finite-width transfer or
first-moment argument closes the census; the departure past $k_\star$ is the
whole phenomenon.
\end{proposition}

The natural first-moment repair also fails, for a structural reason rather than
a computational one. The measured per-row survival rate $\varphi/4\approx0.4045$
is exact, not fitted: $\varphi^k$ counts the hard-core (Fibonacci) language a
surviving continuation must land in, and $4^k$ is the codomain of the
forced-symbol map. If that map were equidistributed, fiber-counting alone would
force $\gm(n)\to\infty$ with no pseudorandomness assumption; it is not
equidistributed, since the map's image saturates at a fixed fraction of $2^n$
well below $4^k$ and its fibers stabilize at a small constant rather than
shrinking, so past $k\sim n/2$ the argument would need a fiber smaller than
one, which is impossible for a nonempty fiber. Both bounds fail for the same
underlying reason: the forcing recursion has genuinely unbounded memory (every
new symbol rewrites the entire stored state), and no bounded-memory model,
spectral or first-moment, can represent an unbounded one at any finite width.

\section{Part III: The periodicity ladder and its limits}
\label{sec:ladder}
% ============================================================================

The ladder encodes ``the center column is eventually $p$-periodic'' as
$\omega$-automaton emptiness over column pairs, with the inverse transduction, a
light-cone wedge, and a Buchi periodicity condition. An EMPTY verdict at some
finite depth would deliver $\mathrm{Thm}(p)$ directly: together with the
classical fact that two adjacent columns cannot both be eventually periodic
\cite[Prop.~3]{jen}\cite{kopra}, no admissible lasso would mean no lone-seed
diagram has an eventually $p$-periodic center column, for any transient
length. Calibration mechanically reproduces Part~I's period-one theorems at
the depths where they apply, and separately passes the Rule~90 soundness
control, which stays NONEMPTY throughout since Rule~90's center column is
genuinely eventually periodic. For $p=2$ the language is nonempty at every
decided depth, and we prove this is not a computational limit but a structural
one: three independent levers of the encoding are each shown incapable of
forcing emptiness, by proof rather than by exhausting compute.

\begin{theorem}[Three inert levers]\label{thm:ladder}
For the $p=2$ ladder: (i) the accepting verdict is uniform in the right depth
$R$ over a range of raw sizes exceeding $5000\times$; (ii) the boundary pin is
sandwiched, $\mathrm{plain}(R+1)\subseteq\mathrm{pin}(R)\subseteq\mathrm{plain}(R)$,
so it decides nothing depth does not; and (iii) the accepting tail system's
bisimulation-class count is \emph{exactly constant} in the left depth $k$
($15$, $20$, $32$ in the three encodings, over a $3068\times$ range of raw
states), because every accepting state lies in the saturated region where the
left-depth constraints are inert. No lever of the ladder can force emptiness.
\end{theorem}

Each lever closes by an exact argument, not only by measurement. The sandwich
$\mathrm{plain}(R+1)\subseteq\mathrm{pin}(R)\subseteq\mathrm{plain}(R)$ holds
because the boundary pin is exactly the solvability condition for the one
column the strip does not model, and is therefore entailed by any genuine
extension one column further out: it can only find, one column early, an
emptiness that depth would find anyway. The left-depth invariance holds
because the ladder's own saturation rule stops checking the light-cone wedge
once the letter count passes a fixed threshold, so every column added beyond
that threshold affects neither of the two values the automaton returns; the
accepting tail therefore lives entirely in a region where left depth is
provably inert, not merely observed to be flat. Both closures apply equally to
the Rule~90 control, which is what keeps the soundness check alive: Rule~90's
own extendability condition is vacuous, so its ladder is the plain language and
never inherits Rule~30's pin.

\begin{proposition}[The witness-extension test is invalid]\label{prop:witness}
Extracting a ladder witness and testing whether it extends to a genuine
lone-seed diagram cannot decide anything: on the Rule~90 control, where a genuine
witness provably exists, the sampler misses it by a factor of four in depth. The
nonemptiness is attributable to the free boundary of the truncated strip
(obstruction~F).
\end{proposition}

The Rule~90 control is what condemns the test, not a property of the Rule~30
sample. A genuine, fully realizable Rule~90 witness exists at onset one and
extends to the probe limit, yet randomized sampling of the accepting
component never finds anything within a factor of four of it, at every tested
depth. Since the sampler already fails on an object known to be genuine, its
failure on the Rule~30 candidates carries no information about whether those
candidates are spurious.

Ninety-four distinct routes have been attempted against

\begin{description}
\item[A. The $O(\log t)$ propagation wall.] Trace-anchored analysis determines a
  region of depth only $O(\log t)$, from one-bit-per-step information loss; three
  independent representations hit it. \emph{Type:} depth. Does not bind the
  period-two counting question, which uses the periodicity hypothesis globally.
  The rate is a statement about the \emph{right} light cone, where the local
  rule reduces to a running parity and the diagonal periods double. The left
  cone reaches far deeper for the same period budget and A should not be quoted
  there with this rate; see Remark~\ref{rem:wedge}.
\item[B. The Rule~90 filter.] Any argument using only left permutivity,
  additivity, or expansivity proves a false statement about Rule~90.
  \emph{Type:} rule-generic. The organizing obstruction of the whole paper.
\item[C. Single-column blindness.] Entropy- and complexity-boundary framings
  measure a two-dimensional field insensitive to any one column. \emph{Type:}
  population/observable. Kills the Kolmogorov and block-entropy routes.
\item[D. The missing composition law.] Every proposed Problem~3 shortcut needs a
  composition law it never supplies. \emph{Type:} composition.
\item[E. The measure-zero single-orbit gap.] Rule~30 is ergodic for Bernoulli
  measure, so almost-everywhere density results never see the lone seed.
  \emph{Type:} measure. Kills the density routes to Problem~2.
\item[F. The free boundary of a fixed-depth strip.] Any fixed-depth column window
  leaves its outermost column unconstrained, and the phase slip relocates there.
  \emph{Type:} boundary. The ladder's wall (Part~III).
\item[G. Arbitrary-input measures versus a fixed point.] Circuit size, decision
  depth, sensitivity and the like are identically zero on one fixed input.
  \emph{Type:} object-mismatch. Kills the Problem~3 complexity routes.
\item[H. Finite certificates are complexity lower bounds, never more.] Any
  bounded exclusion or census bounds a complexity function and cannot become an
  all-length theorem. \emph{Type:} intrinsic. Why no census closes a theorem.
\item[I. The pinned light-cone CNF.] With $n$ fixed, the circuit CNF refutes only
  that instance. \emph{Type:} object-mismatch. Kills the pinned Problem~3 SAT.
\end{description}

\begin{remark}[A named gap: non-invertibility]

\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The ordered wedge, and why obstruction~A has two faces]
\label{rem:wedge}
Write the local rule in each light-cone frame. With $R_j[t]=s(t,t-j)$ one gets
$R_j[t]=R_j[t-1]\oplus(R_{j-1}[t-1]\vee R_{j-2}[t-1])$, in which $R_j$ is the
running parity of a function of strictly smaller indices; this is the induction
behind the power-of-two right-diagonal periods, and behind obstruction~A. With
$L_j[t]=s(t,-t+j)$ one gets instead
\[
  L_j[t]\;=\;L_{j-2}[t-1]\;\oplus\;\bigl(L_{j-1}[t-1]\vee L_j[t-1]\bigr),
\]
where the \textsc{or} is self-referential and absorbing: when $L_j[t-1]=1$ the
update collapses to $L_j[t]=\neg L_{j-2}[t-1]$ and the middle argument is
discarded. The two frames behave oppositely, and the left one is the tame side.

That the left region is ordered is not new: \cite[p.~871]{nks} records that
$L_j$ is eventually periodic with the first occurrence of period
$2,4,8,16,32,64$ at depth $3,\,8,\,29,\,400,\,87\,867$ and $2\,107\,985\,255$ or
more. We reproduce that table independently, to the unit, and it is what pins
the identification of $L_j$ with Wolfram's diagonal $j$ cells in from the left
edge. What the frame adds is the recurrence above, which makes the asymmetry
mechanical rather than pictorial, and the observation that left-frame
periodicity of period $g$ to depth $D$ is exactly the spacetime isometry
\[
  s(t+g,\,x-g)=s(t,x)\qquad\text{on } -t\le x\le -t+D,
\]
verified directly in $(t,x)$ coordinates on $5.5\times10^{6}$ cells with no
exception, for $g$ a multiple of $16$ and for no other $g$ tested. In that form
the ordered region is the same kind of object as the periodic tori censused in
Part~III, which is the only structural bridge between them we know.

Two consequences for the register. First, obstruction~A is a statement about the
\emph{right} cone. With period at most $P$ the right frame reaches depth
$\approx 2.4\log_2 P$, while the left reaches $3,8,29,400,87\,867,\ldots$ ---
super-exponential in $\log P$. A's verdict survives, since both still demand
unbounded period at depth $\Theta(t)$, but its rate is wrong on the left and
should not be quoted there. Second, and this is why the remark belongs in a
paper whose unifying fact is the Rule~90 filter: replacing $\vee$ by $\oplus$
destroys the phenomenon outright. The Rule~90 left frame is a running parity
like its right one, its measured period is exactly $j$, and it has no ordered
region and no order/disorder boundary. In this frame the \textsc{or} is what
creates order rather than what destroys it.

We record two cautions with it, both learned the hard way here. The measured
region is ordered in \emph{time} and aperiodic in \emph{space} --- its rows
carry no spatial period up to $2048$ on bands of width $2737$ --- so it is
invisible in a plot. And a scan of it stopping at $j=65\,536$ shows an unbroken
period-$16$ plateau, which is not a law: the plateau ends at $j=87\,867$. This
is obstruction~H in the form that is easiest to miss, since a plateau, unlike a
certificate, looks like a theorem and costs nothing to state.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The five sightings of the needle]
The collapse of Observation~\ref{obs:needle} was recorded five times before,
under five names, and dropped each time for a kill reason of type~C or E --- a
population statistic --- when the deciding object was never the population but the
$O(1)$ extremal states. This is the methodological lesson of the register.
\end{remark}

\section{Part V: Scope, the unifying fact, and open problems}
\label{sec:scope}
% ============================================================================

Problems~1, 2 and 3 are open and nothing here changes that. Part~I settles
eventual period one completely, for a class strictly larger than the lone seed,
and is the paper's one unconditional advance on the center-column question.
Part~III's limitation theorems close a specific mechanized route to period two.
Part~II's census and lead delimit period two without deciding it. The unifying
fact is the Rule~90 filter: the \textsc{or} nonlinearity is the entire
difference, every proven result routes through it, and every failed route does
not.

Remark~\ref{rem:wedge} sharpens that statement. The \textsc{or} has two branches
and they are not two aspects of one behaviour: in the right light-cone frame it
feeds a running parity and the diagonal periods double every $2.4$ steps of
depth, while in the left frame it is self-referential and absorbing and the
period doubles only at depths $3,8,29,400,87\,867,\ldots$, giving an ordered
region Rule~90 does not have. So the unifying fact is better stated as: the
\textsc{or} makes Rule~30 \emph{two-phased}, and the center column is the one
place the prize question asks about that lies in the disordered phase. We record
this as a reorientation, not a result. No proof of the glide identity is offered;
the ordered region's period is unbounded, only very slowly so; and we know of no
argument that carries a left-region theorem rightward across the interface --- and
no measured centre-only propagation has bridged the gap from the other
side.\footnote{A run of ones is sufficient but \emph{not} necessary for
centre-only forcing: $c(t)=\cdots=c(t+k-1)=1$ forces
$\mathrm{col}_{-k}(t)=c(t+k)\oplus(k\bmod 2)$, but of the $256$ length-$8$
centre words, $116$ force a cell deeper than their run-of-ones prefix. The
shortest witness is $01010$, which forces $\mathrm{col}_{-4}=0$ while leaving
depths $1,2,3$ free; the mechanism is the relation $b\,d=0$ between two
\emph{unknown} right-column cells $b=\mathrm{col}_1(t)$, $d=\mathrm{col}_1(t+2)$,
which a known/unknown propagation cannot see. Rule~90 leaves
$\mathrm{col}_{-4}$ free on the same word, so this is not generic left
permutivity. Exact reach at a fixed depth $k$ is decided by enumerating the
$2^k$ initial right rows; on the lone seed the maxima are $15,27,27,31,31$ over
horizons $t<100,500,1000,2000,4096$ with depths tested through $63$, attained at
times whose run length is $0$ or $1$. Two earlier drafts are retracted: a
``one-column reach'' that saturated the wrong argument of the \textsc{or}, and
an ``exactly when'' whose $O(\log t)$ consequence does not follow. We claim no
asymptotic reach bound here; the finite tables are evidence, not a growth law.}

The concrete open leads are three, one fewer than when this paper was first
drafted: the comparison asking whether the extremal forcing states of
Observation~\ref{obs:needle} form a bounded-period family across $n$ has been
run and is negative, as recorded after Conjecture~\ref{conj:needle}, so the
fixed-family cocycle argument it would have enabled is not in range. What
remains is a
Rule~90 carry-kernel census as the missing soundness control on the period-two
thread; the separator SEP itself, the one open arrow of \eqref{eq:chain}; and
a second, independent route to SEP via mortality of an exact reversed-diagonal
queue construction. On that route, every fixed-radius end window is proved
nonclosed as a transition congruence, however it is augmented by queue
length, endpoint/event data, or the exact total dihedral action of the
interior symbols: two interior blocks collapsing to the same group element
are not dynamically interchangeable, and no bounded summary of that kind can
tell them apart. The one candidate escape that specifically avoids this
obstruction, a finer congruence retaining the full intermediate scan trace
rather than its group-collapsed summary, turns out to close the family rather
than escape it: beyond an exhaustive census of $3{,}015{,}168$ words showing no
class merges, it is injective at every length, since each symbol acts on the
scan states as a permutation and distinct normalized symbols act differently
somewhere. The induced right congruence is therefore the identity and the block
monoid is free, so no bounded lossless summary of an interior block exists at
any radius, of any shape.
Two further candidate escapes (a paired/relative invariant over synchronized
copies; a monotone potential not built from any finite quotient) were tested
and fail.

The same route's remaining lemma has since been reduced twice and then closed as
a mechanism. The lemma needed is the pair of bounds $s_2(W)\le n$,
$s_3(W)\le n+1$ on the forced continuation length of a hard-core source of
length $n$. A deterministic greedy matching certifies it through length~22; the
greedy construction can be weakened to a per-row sensitivity claim, and that in
turn holds after quotienting the newest-cut group by its centre, which for this
$D_8$ coincides with the commutator subgroup. The resulting two-bit invariant is
exactly the $(\alpha,\beta)$ pair of the existing affine coordinates, with the
quadratic coordinate $\gamma$ the kernel and never required; neither bit alone
suffices, so two is the exact size. That strengthened claim holds on $72{,}351$
legal rows through source length~21, clearing the length at which an earlier
pointwise-derivative certificate broke, and is tight on exactly two words.
Nevertheless the reachability form of the claim does not localize. The set of
suffix configurations whose invariant freezes along the nested chain is
nonempty, sparse, and never legal, but the freeze is independent of the prefix
--- every scenario in the chain zeroes it --- while the continuation length
depends on the prefix and its first forced symbol is almost entirely a function
of it. Ten candidate local mechanisms relating the two were refuted by explicit
witnesses, and on the restricted class the bound required is strictly stronger
than the target itself. This is a further instance of obstruction~D, the missing
composition law: there is no composition law for the continuation length, so
concatenation cannot be made to lower it. This route is reported for
completeness and is not otherwise used above.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}
% ============================================================================

All computational claims are reproduced by scripts in the accompanying archive,
seeded and cross-checked; the exhaustive audits and SMT certificates supplement
but do not replace the unbounded proofs of Part~I. The census
(\texttt{fastdk\_core.py}) and ladder (\texttt{run\_rung0.py},
\texttt{rung1.py}) ship with their own cross-checks against brute force or an
independent implementation; the needle analysis is cross-checked the same way
and detailed in its result document. The measurements of Remark~\ref{rem:wedge}
are produced by \texttt{ordered\_wedge.py}, which computes the same object twice
--- once by the plain update in $(t,x)$ and once by each moving-frame recurrence
--- and whose test module asserts that the two agree cell by cell, that no glide
length outside the multiples of $16$ has a wedge, and that the Rule~90 control
fails the bounded-period claim. The full
corpus --- scripts, logs, and the result documents underlying the register ---
