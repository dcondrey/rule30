# Quarter-wave coherence: finite-support loss and inverse persistence

The spatial variance recursion gives two further exact inequalities. Finite
support supplies an explicit positive loss floor. Small contrast, conversely,
forces quarter-wave energy to survive on longer windows. Neither inequality
proves the accumulated loss required on the Rule 30 lone-seed orbit. A separate
local history certificate below connects near-maximal window energy to eight
subsequent Rule 30 steps, without assuming a random background.

## 1. Definitions and the finite-support loss floor

Fix a nonzero binary positive row x_1,...,x_t, extended by zero for j>t.
For L>=1 define

\[
B_{L,j}=\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}i^r x_{j+r},\quad
E_L=\sum_{j\ge1}|B_{L,j}|^2,\quad V_L=\frac{E_L}{tL^2},
\]

\[
Q_L=\sum_{j=1}^L|B_{L,j}|^2,\quad
D_L=\sum_{j\ge1}|B_{L,j}-i^L B_{L,j+L}|^2,\quad
\delta_L=\frac{2Q_L+D_L}{4E_L}.
\]

The [variance identity](RESULTS-finite-gap-mps-and-variance.md) is
E_(2L)=4E_L-2Q_L-D_L and V_(2L)=V_L(1-delta_L).
All complex operations here are ordinary arithmetic, not GF(2).

With n=ceil(t/L), finite support alone gives

\[
\boxed{\delta_L\ge
\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2n+1}\right).} \tag{1}
\]

To prove this, set A_(L,j)=i^j B_(L,j), and let S shift by L toward the
zero tail: (SA)_j=A_(j+L). Then A_(2L)=(I+S)A_L. Split the vector into
its L residue classes of j modulo L. Each resulting chain has length at
most n. On a chain of length m, the matrix (I+S)^*(I+S) is tridiagonal,
with diagonal (1,2,...,2) and both adjacent diagonals equal to one.
Its characteristic polynomials satisfy

\[
p_0(\lambda)=1,\quad p_1(\lambda)=\lambda-1,\quad
p_m(\lambda)=(\lambda-2)p_{m-1}(\lambda)-p_{m-2}(\lambda).
\]

Substitution gives the exact Laurent-polynomial identity

\[
p_m(2+z+z^{-1})=\sum_{r=-m}^m z^r.
\]

Its m distinct eigenvalues are
2+2 cos(2k pi/(2m+1)), k=1,...,m. Hence the largest is
4 cos^2(pi/(2m+1)), which increases with m. Summing the chain estimates
gives E_(2L)<=4 cos^2(pi/(2n+1)) E_L and proves (1).
This is a sharp operator-norm bound for arbitrary complex chain vectors;
no sharpness claim is made for window vectors generated by binary rows.

For every actual lone-seed row at t>=1 the bound applies, since x_t^t=1.
At mesoscopic scales L=o(t), its right side is asymptotic to
pi^2 L^2/(4t^2). In fact, for dyadic L_j<=L_J=o(t), the sum of these
guaranteed floors is O((L_J/t)^2), tending to zero. Thus the floor cannot
supply a diverging accumulated loss before windows reach the row length.
For L>=t, (1) gives the exact value delta_L=3/4.

## 2. Small contrast forces persistence to longer scales

For every integer m>=1,

\[
A_{mL}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}S^k A_L,\qquad
D_L=\|(I-S)A_L\|_2^2.
\]

Because S is a contraction, telescoping and the triangle inequality give

\[
\|A_{mL}-mA_L\|_2
\le\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k\sqrt{D_L}
=\frac{m(m-1)}2\sqrt{D_L}.
\]

Writing epsilon_L=D_L/E_L and [u]_+=max(u,0), this proves

\[
\boxed{V_{mL}\ge V_L
\left[1-\frac{m-1}{2}\sqrt{\epsilon_L}\right]_+^2
\ge V_L[1-(m-1)\sqrt{\delta_L}]_+^2.} \tag{2}
\]

The second inequality uses epsilon_L<=4 delta_L. In particular,
m-1<=1/sqrt(epsilon_L) guarantees V_(mL)>=V_L/4. Low contrast aligns
the aggregated quarter-wave amplitudes across shifts of length L; it
preserves their energy up to a scale of order L/sqrt(epsilon_L).
It does not imply that the underlying binary word is approximately
periodic.

Equivalently, for m>=2 an independently established drop
V_(mL)<=rho^2 V_L with 0<=rho<1 would certify

\[
\epsilon_L\ge\frac{4(1-\rho)^2}{(m-1)^2},\qquad
\delta_L\ge\frac{(1-\rho)^2}{(m-1)^2}. \tag{3}
\]

This is a possible history-certificate target, not a proof that the
required drop occurs on the seed.

For a concrete structural consequence, suppose 4 divides L, V_L>=v>0,
and m-1<=1/sqrt(epsilon_L). Put M=mL. Every binary M-window obeys
|B_(M,j)|^2<=M^2/8, while (2) gives E_M>=v t M^2/4. Therefore at least
v t of its window starts j=1,...,t obey

\[
|B_{M,j}|^2\ge(v/8)M^2.
\]

Indeed, if g is the number of such starts, the complementary upper bound
E_M<=[g+(t-g)v]M^2/8 gives g>=v t/(1-v)>=v t.
At each such start, one of the two opposite-residue count differences
has magnitude at least sqrt(v) M/4. Small loss together with substantial
energy thus forces many long windows with substantial residue-count bias.
Ruling out these windows often enough on the seed remains an additional
dynamical problem.

## 3. A local Rule 30 history certificate near maximal energy

There is a stronger geometric conclusion when a window's energy is close
to its maximum, rather than merely bounded below. Let 4 divide L, and
let n_a count its ones at relative positions congruent to a modulo four.
Set a=n_0-n_2 and b=n_1-n_3, so |B|^2=a^2+b^2. Its exact minimum
Hamming distance e to the four repeated templates

```
1100   0110   0011   1001
```

is

\[
e=L/2-|a|-|b|.
\]

Each template chooses one member from each pair of opposite residue
classes. Writing h=L/4 and factoring h^2-a^2 and h^2-b^2 gives

\[
\boxed{e\le\frac4L\left(\frac{L^2}{8}-|B|^2\right).} \tag{4}
\]

Under Rule 30, the spatially periodic row 1100 enters the following
eight-cycle after one step:

```
1011 -> 0010 -> 0111 -> 0100 -> 1110 -> 1000 -> 1101 -> 0001
  ^                                                        |
  +--------------------------------------------------------+
```

Each fixed spatial column contains four ones in these eight rows; rotating
the initial template only rotates the cycle spatially. Consequently, if a
window at any time on the actual seed orbit is e bits from a template,
trim eight sites from each end. Every remaining column whose distance
from all e mismatches exceeds eight has exactly four ones in its next
eight time steps. This follows directly from radius-one causality and the
displayed cycle.

At most 17e columns can lie within distance eight of those mismatches.
Writing I for the trimmed interval and using c_i(s)=x_i^(t+s), one obtains
the exact local history estimate

\[
\boxed{\left|\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{s=1}^{8}(1-2c_i(s))\right|
\le136e.} \tag{5}
\]

The statement is valid for the actual seed whenever its window satisfies
the stated premise, and for every other Rule 30 orbit. It gives a
deterministic way to turn sufficiently strong spatial coherence into a
short balanced history, rather than presuming that coherence must decay.
It establishes neither how often the premise holds on the seed nor a
temporal bound for its designated center column. In particular, the low
contrast premise of (2) alone does not imply the near-maximal premise
needed for (4).

## 4. Verification and precise remaining scope

The independent integer
[verifier](../../experiments/rule30/quarter_wave_coherence_audit.py) checks
the variance and persistence identities on finite binary rows, the
Laurent-polynomial characteristic identity, the exact Hamming-distance
formula, and the eight-cycle with radius-one perturbation tests. It uses
Gaussian-integer pairs for all window sums; numerical trigonometry is not
used to prove (1). The run checked 4,554 finite-row/scale cases, 22,770
persistence cases, 32 characteristic-polynomial identities, 272 Hamming
windows, and 388 perturbed histories, all successfully.

The missing seed-specific input is still an all-time estimate that rules
out persistent residue bias, or otherwise controls its frequency and its
interaction with the accumulated nonlinear current. Finite origin alone
does not provide that input: the coherent finite-origin examples in the
variance report remain valid. None of (1)--(5) proves or disproves P1, P2,
or P3. Even a successful estimate P_t=o(t) would settle only the spatial
quarter-wave part of the current decomposition; P2 also requires control
of the accumulated current.
