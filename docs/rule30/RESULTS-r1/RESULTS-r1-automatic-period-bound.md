# An exact automaticity test for candidate R1 counterdiagrams

Date: 2026-09-09. **Intermediate construction tool. R1 and P1 remain open.**

The direct automatic-diagram search can leave its proposed neighbour signal
unspecified and still certify aperiodicity with one finite inequality. The
essential prerequisite is an independently verified finite automaton defining
that signal at every time. A mismatch in a simulated Rule 30 trace alone does
not have this consequence.

## Evidence and scope

* **U:** The period and onset bound below, proved symbolically and independently
  audited.
* **C:** A finite addition-product automaton either exhibits a mismatch or checks
  the stated equality at every integer above the bound.
* **C/M:** Exhaustive checks of all reachable labelled padded binary-output
  automata with one through three states, plus independent scalar regressions.
* No Rule 30 candidate is asserted to exist. This generic sequence lemma is an
  observable test, not a Rule 30 dynamical argument.

## 1. Definitions

Let a deterministic binary-output automaton have `N>=1` reachable states, root
`q0`, transitions `delta_0,delta_1`, and output `tau`. It reads the binary
digits of a nonnegative integer from **least significant to most significant**.
The empty representation of zero is permitted. Require

```text
tau(delta_0(q)) = tau(q)
```

at every reachable state. Appending arbitrarily many high zero digits therefore
preserves the output. Write `u_q(n)` for the sequence from state `q`; then

```text
u_q(2n+b) = u_(delta_b(q))(n),       b in {0,1}.
```

Different states may define the same sequence; minimization is unnecessary.

## 2. Uniform period bound

**Theorem (U).** Define

```text
H = 2^N,       P = 2^N * lcm(1,2,...,N).
```

Then `u_(q0)` is eventually periodic if and only if

```text
u_(q0)(n+P) = u_(q0)(n)   for every n >= H.
```

Consequently a single exact inequality at `n>=H` proves this particular
automatically defined sequence is not eventually periodic.

**Proof.** Sufficiency is the definition of eventual periodicity. Suppose the
root sequence is eventually periodic. Every reachable state is a decimation
of that sequence and is eventually periodic too. Let `p_q` be the least period
of its eventual tail, and write `p_q=2^(a_q)*b_q`, with `b_q` odd.

First, if `a_q>0`, both child tail periods divide `p_q/2`, and at least one
child has exponent exactly `a_q-1`. For `a_q=1` both child exponents are zero.
For `a_q>=2`, if both were smaller than `a_q-1`, interleaving their tails would
give the parent a period with smaller 2-adic exponent, contradicting minimality
of `p_q`. Following a child with the maximal exponent visits states with
distinct exponents `a_q,a_q-1,...,0`. Thus `a_q<=N-1`.

Next consider a state whose least tail period `d` is odd. Choose `e` with
`2^e=1 mod d` and `2^e>=d`. Its depth-`e` decimations include all sequences
`u_q(2^e*n+j)`, `0<=j<d`. Their tails are the `d` different cyclic shifts of a
primitive period-`d` word, hence require at least `d` distinct states. Therefore
`d<=N`.

After `a_q` decimations, every descendant tail period is odd and divides `b_q`.
Let `D` be their least common multiple. Reinterleaving gives the parent a tail
period `2^(a_q)*D`, so minimality also gives `b_q|D`. Conversely each descendant
period divides `b_q`, so `D=b_q`. Each descendant period is at most `N`; hence
`b_q` divides `lcm(1,...,N)`. All tail periods therefore divide `P`.

It remains to bound the transient. Extend each eventual periodic tail uniquely
to a purely periodic sequence `v_q(n)` for all `n>=0`. The identities

```text
v_q(2n+b) = v_(delta_b(q))(n)
```

hold first for all sufficiently large `n`, and then for every `n`, because both
sides are purely periodic. Thus the `v_q` are generated by the same transitions
with the output at state `q` changed to `v_q(0)`. The difference `u_q XOR v_q`
is generated by the same `N` states with output `tau(q) XOR v_q(0)`, and has
finite support.

A finite-support sequence generated by a padded `N`-state LSD automaton cannot
be one at an integer whose canonical binary representation has more than `N`
digits. Such a representation ends in a most-significant `1`. Among the more
than `N` state positions before that final digit, two states repeat. Pump the
intervening digit block, keeping the final high `1`. The resulting canonical
words describe unbounded integers with the same terminal state and output,
contradicting finite support. Every discrepancy is therefore below `2^N`.
For `n>=H`, the sequence equals its purely periodic extension, whose period
divides `P`. This proves the theorem. QED.

The bounds are deliberately conservative; no minimality is claimed.

## 3. Exact finite checker

The checker reads the digits of `n` and `n+P` simultaneously. Its state retains
the two automaton states, the addition carry, a digit position capped after
all digits of `P` have been read, and whether a `1` at position at least `N`
has occurred. The last condition is exactly `n>=H`. Beyond the cap, all fixed
digits of `P` are zero, so the product is finite and stationary.

An accepting state has exhausted `P`, has zero carry, and has different output
bits. Breadth-first search then supplies an actual integer witness, replayed
with separate scalar evaluations of `u(n)` and `u(n+P)`. If no accepting state
is reachable, every integer `n>=H` satisfies the equality. This is an exhaustive
finite product calculation, not a bounded scan of time indices.

## 4. Most-significant-digit automata

The state count in the theorem must not be confused with the state count of a
most-significant-digit automaton. An `n`-state binary-output MSD automaton can
be reversed to a padded LSD automaton on at most `2^n` Boolean functions
`f:Q->{0,1}`. Start at `f=tau`, update

```text
f -> f composed with delta_b,
```

and output `f(q0)`. If the original root has a leading-zero self-loop, high-zero
padding preserves this output at every reversed state. This construction is
exact and need not attain all `2^n` states. Use the actual reachable reversed
state count, or the safe bound `N=2^n`, in the theorem. Additional clocks or
masked subsequence encodings require their own justified state accounting.

## 5. Regression and controls

The exhaustive enumeration covers labelled automata with root zero, every
state reachable, and the padding identity at every state:

The classifications use the proved theorem and exact product; the independent
scalar checks comprise 241,358 comparisons, without extrapolation from those
comparisons. Four named formulas are independently checked at 4,096 integers
each. Thue–Morse has the exact certificate `N=2,H=4,P=8,n=4`. Valuation parity
`v2(n+1) mod 2` has `N=4,H=16,P=192,n=319`. The finite exceptional sequence
`[n=0]` passes the eventual-periodicity product, as required.

The actual Rule 90 lone-seed neighbour has the four-state certificate
`H=16,P=192,n=31`: its values at 31 and 223 differ. Its formula
`[n>0 and n=2^j-1 for some j>=1]` is checked against 256 neighbour samples
from the frozen ladder truth table, comprising 66,048 cell updates. The
unchanged `controls.rule90_control(6)` also passes its three cases.

This lemma is deliberately rule-independent. It must accept aperiodic Rule 90
observables as well as hypothetical Rule 30 observables. Its role is to certify
aperiodicity **after** the different Rule 30 local constraints have been
independently checked; it supplies no replacement for the OR-dependent part of
an R1 proof or construction.

## 6. A complete finite mismatch search for MSD automata

**Lemma (U).** Let `u` be generated by an `n`-state MSD automaton whose root has
a leading-zero self-loop. Fix `P>=1` and `H>=0`, and choose `L` so that
`P<2^L` and `H<=2^L`. If some integer `z>=H` satisfies `u(z)!=u(z+P)`, then
such an integer exists with at most `L+4*n^2` binary digits.

**Proof.** Write

```text
z = 2^L*m+r,       z+P = 2^L*(m+c)+r',
0<=r,r'<2^L,      c in {0,1}.
```

Read the high parts `m,m+c` synchronously from most significant digits,
padding their representations to a common length. Retain the two automaton
states, the current prefix difference `d`, and whether a `1` has occurred in
the representation of `m`. Every prefix difference is in `{0,1}`: a negative
prefix difference can never be repaired to a nonnegative final difference,
and a prefix difference at least two can never be repaired to zero or one.
The transition on digit pair `(b,b')` updates `d` to `2*d+b'-b`, rejecting
values outside `{0,1}`. This is a graph on at most `4*n^2` states.

The original high parts reach some endpoint whose difference is `c`. Replace
their path by a shortest path to that same endpoint. It has fewer than
`4*n^2` edges. Let its represented integers be `m0,m1`; then `m1=m0+c`,
both resulting automaton states are unchanged, and `m0>0` holds exactly when
`m>0`. Append the original `L` low digits `r,r'`. The new full integers still
differ by `P`, and their output bits are the same as the original two output
bits, hence differ. If `m>0`, the new first integer is at least `2^L>=H`.
If `m=0`, the replacement also has `m0=0`, and the original value `r>=H` is
retained. The new integer has at most `L+4*n^2` digits. QED.

Combined with the conservative reversal bound, take `M=2^n`,
`H=2^M`, `P=2^M*lcm(1,...,M)`, and `L=bit_length(P)`. Searching all mismatch
integers of at most `L+4*n^2` bits is then **complete** for aperiodicity of the
specified `n`-state MSD observable. A smaller implementation cap is only a
restricted search, and its UNSAT result must retain that qualification.
