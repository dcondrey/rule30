# Exact catalogue identification and the applicable reset-group normal form

Date: 2026-09-14. **The original binary automaton is catalogue automaton
2369 after renaming its states. Its group is a locally finite 2-group
extended by an infinite cyclic group.** The structural statement is a
specialization of established reset-automaton theory, not a new group
classification. It does not provide a fast singleton query.

## 1. Identification without changing the action

Write the original binary maps as A0,B0,C0, reserving unadorned letters
in other reports for their itinerary conjugates. Their input-state recursions are

```
A0=(A0,C0),       B0=swap(A0,C0),       C0=swap(B0,C0).
```

Bondarenko et al.'s catalogue uses `g(xw)=root_g(x) section_g,x(w)`.
Set `(a,b,c)=(C0,B0,A0)`. The resulting recursion is exactly its
automaton2369; the original ordering has number5002, and the catalogue
table also identifies5002 with2369. The entry records a noncontracting,
self-replicating action and lists some relations, not a complete group
presentation. See the numbering formula on printed page14, the table on
page40, and entry2369 on page94 of the
[classification paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.3555).
No bit complement, inverse, change of origin, or conjugacy is involved
in this relabeling. Our chronological products must nevertheless be
reversed when compared with ordinary function-composition products.

There is an applicable structural theorem beyond that catalogue label.
Group two consecutive binary input letters, with numeric value
`x0+2*x1`. For **every** starting state, the resulting section is

| Input digit | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Section | A0 | B0 | C0 | C0 |

Thus the blocked machine is an invertible reset automaton. Grouping bits
is faithful on the boundary, so it preserves the generated group.
Silva and Steinberg's reset-automaton theorem supplies the locally-finite-by-cyclic
structure; see [On a class of automata groups generalizing lamplighter groups](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218196705002761).
The theorem does not identify every such group with an ordinary lamplighter
group, or give an efficient representation of the actual power query.

## 2. A direct specialization with an explicit finite localization

Here all compositions are ordinary, with the rightmost map applied first.
Let A=A0 and define the complete boundary permutations

```
tau(x)=x XOR1,
sigma(x)=4*floor(x/4)+((x-1) mod4).
```

Then `B0=tau composed with A` and `C0=sigma composed with A`.
The group D generated by tau and sigma is the eight-element binary-tree
group at depth2. The existing
[ordered-defect proof](RESULTS-p3-conjugated-defects.md) gives, for j>=0,

```
Ad_A^j(D) changes no bit at position >=2+2j,
Ad_A^j(d)=A^j composed with d composed with A^(-j).             (1)
```

The reason is finite propagation: a difference initially confined to the
first two bits can advance at most two places in each forward A step.
Compatibility also makes the changed prefix independent of the untouched
tail. Consequently (1) places this conjugate inside a finite binary-tree
permutation group, as complete maps on all infinite inputs.

Put

```
G=<A,D>,       N=<Ad_A^j(D): j in Z>.
```

N is normal in G. Given any finite family of its elements, expand their
finite expressions into conjugated D factors and let [p,q] contain all
indices used. Conjugating the entire family by A^(-p) changes the indices
to [0,q-p]. By(1), the resulting subgroup lies in the finite binary-tree
group of depth

```
w=2+2(q-p).                                                   (2)
```

It is a finite 2-group. Conjugation preserves finiteness and element
orders, so every finitely generated subgroup of N is a finite 2-group.
In particular, every element of N is torsion.

A has infinite order: for every positive integer x, its formula
`A(x)=x XOR((x<<1) OR(x<<2))` raises the highest nonzero bit by exactly2.
Therefore `<A> intersect N={1}`. Since G=N<A>, this proves

```
G=N semidirect <A>,       <A> is isomorphic to Z.              (3)
```

The quotient map chi:G->Z sends each of A0,B0,C0 to1. Thus the total
signed generator count is well-defined, and an element belongs to N
exactly when that count is zero. Every element has a unique decomposition
`n composed with A^k` with n in N and k in Z. The expression of n as an
ordered list of defects is not unique; (3) asserts uniqueness of the
group elements n and k, not of a syntax or efficient normal-form encoding.

Every binary section preserves chi. A section of a signed generator is
one signed generator with the same sign; applying the ordered section
composition law to a word therefore preserves its signed count. It
follows in particular that even degree is a section-closed condition.
Every section of B0^N still has degree N and needs at least N letters in
an ordinary signed-generator word. This statement does not charge a
power-expression node as N letters or exclude a fast evaluator for one.

Equation(2) is an effective bound for a supplied defect expression.
An automorphism of the binary tree of depth w has order dividing2^w:
induct on w, squaring first if its root action swaps the two subtrees.
Hence a zero-degree expression localized by(2) also has order dividing2^w.
The finite group itself can be very large. The bound does not authorize
enumerating it without a separate compute justification.

## 3. Computational consequence and the operation still missing

The verifier constructs signed-word defect expressions and compares
them with independent raw binary Mealy scans. This detects the cyclic
degree and gives a finite localization of each supplied zero-degree
correction. The elementary implementation shifts a list at every letter,
so its expression construction costs O(L^2) elementary list operations
for L supplied letters; integer-index costs are additional. It does not
claim that a binary power exponent represents L supplied letters for free.

For positive B0 powers the degree is N, and the normal form is precisely

```
B0^N = tau Ad_A(tau) ... Ad_A^(N-1)(tau) A^N.                 (4)
```

The product uses ordinary composition in the displayed order. Since
A^N(0)=0, evaluating the correction product at0 already evaluates
B0^N(0). For N>=1 it acts within the first2N-1 bits, using the sharper
one-bit support of tau. Neither the finite support nor the locally
finite kernel makes that growing ordered product cheap to evaluate.
The itinerary change of coordinates and the marked readout must also
remain charged whenever they are used. The actual singleton demand
`bit_e(digit_(h-1)(B^(h+1+e)(0)))` is therefore unresolved.

The constructive next obligation is specific: aggregate the ordered
conjugates in(4) at the demanded precision with a proved cost saving.
Ordinary lamplighter collection is not supplied by(3); the
[existing exact noncommutation witness](RESULTS-p3-conjugated-defects.md)
already shows that these conjugates cannot simply be commuted. Conversely,
noncontraction and amenability imply no lower bound for this query.
