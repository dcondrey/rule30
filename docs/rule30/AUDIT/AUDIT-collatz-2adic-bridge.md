**Collatz and the 2-adic shift conjugacy: scope of the bridge, 2026-09-15**

STATUS: CLOSED as a transfer route. The proposed conjugacy between the
triangular core map `H` and the Collatz map exists, is automatic, and
reaches no prize target. One explicit sufficient arithmetic target for P1
is recorded in section 5 together with its open obligation; no work on
that obligation is reported here. No claim of novelty is made.

This audit checks an externally proposed connection against the primary
papers and the current archive. The repository contained no prior mention
of Collatz, the 3x+1 map, parity vectors, Bernstein or Lagarias before this
file (grep over `docs/`, `experiments/`, `research/`, tracked and untracked,
zero hits; the `3n+1` matches in `RESULTS-r1-c011-seam.md` and
`RESULTS-r1-cantor-return-gap.md` are block-index subscripts).

**1. The proposal.**

A separate session proposed a change of coordinates `J` with

    J(H(x)) = T^2(J(x)),

where `T(x) = x/2` for even `x` and `(3x+1)/2` for odd `x` is the Collatz
map on the 2-adic integers, reported `J(3) = -1`, and offered the
conditional "if the single-seed center column is eventually periodic, then
some positive integer has a Collatz orbit never reaching 1". The last
sentence was not derived and, as section 4 shows, cannot be derived from the
conjugacy.

**2. The conjugacy exists and carries no content.**

Two proved facts:

- [RESULTS-p3-itinerary-conjugacy.md](RESULTS-p3-itinerary-conjugacy.md)
  eq. (5), lines 72-75: `Phi(Hx) = Phi(x) >> 2`, with `Phi` a bijective
  2-adic isometry of all of `Z_2` (eq. (3)-(4), lines 54-69). So `H` is
  topologically conjugate to the full one-sided base-4 shift `S^2`, where
  `S(x) = x >> 1`.
- Lagarias, Amer. Math. Monthly 92 (1985), Theorem L, and Bernstein,
  Proc. AMS 121 (1994); Bernstein and Lagarias, Canad. J. Math. 48 (1996),
  eq. (1.3) and (1.5): the parity-vector map
  `Q(x) = sum_i (T^i(x) mod 2) 2^i` is a measure-preserving homeomorphism
  of `Z_2` with `Q T Q^{-1} = S`. Hence `T^2 = Q^{-1} S^2 Q`.

Conjugacy is transitive, so `J := Q^{-1} Phi` satisfies `J H J^{-1} = T^2`
with nothing further used. `J` is determined only up to the centralizer of
`S^2` (the automorphisms of the one-sided 4-shift).

`J` does map rationals to rationals. The local rule
`(Hx)_j = x_(j+2) XOR (x_(j+1) OR x_j)` (itinerary report eq. (1), line
40) sends a bit sequence with eventual period `q` and preperiod `r` to one
with eventual period dividing `q` and preperiod at most `r`, a finite set,
so a rational `x` has an eventually periodic `H`-orbit and a rational
`Phi(x)`; Corollary 1 in section 4 then makes `Q^{-1}(Phi(x))` rational.
Because `H` is permutive in `x_(j+2)`, the same finite-state argument runs
backwards (a periodic point of `H^P` satisfies an order-`2P` bit recurrence,
and preimages under `H` are generated by a recurrence driven by an
eventually periodic input), so `Phi` restricts to a bijection of
`Q cap Z_2`. Consequently `J` is a bijection of the rationals exactly when
Lagarias's Periodicity Conjecture (section 4) holds. Nothing is preserved
about the positive integers, and the reported value shows it. It is
reproduced by hand and is the expected artifact, not a defect:
`H(3) = (3>>2) XOR ((3>>1) OR 3) = 3`,
so 3 is a fixed point of `H` with constant base-4 itinerary `3,3,3,...`,
`Phi(3) = 3 * sum_t 4^t = -1`, and `-1` is the point whose Collatz parity
vector is all ones, i.e. the fixed point `T(-1) = -1`. Thus `J(3) = -1`
says only that a fixed point went to a fixed point.

What `J` transfers is exact and limited: by construction `Q(J(x)) = Phi(x)`,
so the base-4 itinerary of `x` under `H` is, symbol for symbol, the pairwise
grouped Collatz parity vector of `J(x)`. Period, preperiod and orbit closure
transfer; nothing arithmetic about `J(x)` does.

This does not contradict the obstruction in
[RESULTS-p3-dyadic-sections.md](RESULTS-p3-dyadic-sections.md) lines
129-148 (no prefix-compatible bijection conjugating the seed orbit of `G` to
an odd affine map), since `T` is piecewise affine, not affine, and the
conjugated map is `H = G >> 2`, not `G`. Line 76 of the itinerary report
already states that the `Phi` conjugacy is not one of `G` to an invertible
affine map.

**3. The seed reduction does not present the center column as an itinerary of `H`.**

The singleton reduction, eq. (11), in the itinerary report lines 171-176
reads

    c_(2h)   = bit_1 of digit_(h-1) of Phi(C^(h+1)(0)),   h >= 1,
    c_(2h+1) = bit_0 of digit_h     of Phi(C^(h+1)(0)),   h >= 0,

using `Phi(C^L(0)) = K_C^L(0)` (line 176). The original form in
[RESULTS-p3-bulk-core-formula.md](RESULTS-p3-bulk-core-formula.md) lines
165-167 is `c_(2h) = bit_1(Q^(h-1)(C^(h+1))(0))`,
`c_(2h+1) = bit_0(Q^h(C^(h+1))(0))`, where that file's `Q` is the section
operator, not the parity-vector map `Q` of this file.

Both the point `C^(h+1)(0)` and the digit position move with `h`. Since
`Phi` is a bijection, a point `x_c = Phi^{-1}(s)` whose `H`-itinerary is
the column does exist (with `s` the column read as a 2-adic integer, as in
section 5), and `J(x_c) = xi_30` is exactly the point of section 5. But
`x_c` is defined through the column and no relation between `x_c` and the
seed data `C^(h+1)(0)` is known. In particular `xi_30` is not `J(seed)`:
the singleton seed in bit coordinates is `x = 1`, and
`H(1) = 0 XOR (0 OR 1) = 1`, `Phi(1) = sum_t 4^t = -1/3`, whose bits
`1,0,1,0,...` are the parity vector of the trivial Collatz cycle `{1,2}`, so
`J(1) = 1`. The seed is a fixed point on both sides and its `J`-image
describes nothing about the column. As computed from the seed, the column is a
diagonal read across a family of `H`-orbits, the mechanism that killed the
2-adic ergodicity arm:
[overnight/RESULTS-anashin.md](overnight/RESULTS-anashin.md) kill 3 (lines
39-51, the odometer `x -> x+1` with `bit_t(t)` identically 0; equidistribution
of one orbit does not control a diagonal read). The bounded-index theorem
there (lines 53-66) requires a 1-Lipschitz map and neither `H` (eq. (2),
line 48: `v_2` drops by 2 per step) nor `T` (`v_2` drops by 1 on each
branch) is 1-Lipschitz, so it neither applies nor is needed: the
bounded-index read of the orbit of `x_c` (digit `t` of `Phi(x_c)` is
`H^t(x_c) mod 4`) exists and forces nothing, because `x_c` has no
description independent of `s`. What `J` transfers is therefore the
tautology of section 5 and nothing about the seed.

**4. Direction of the periodicity theorem.**

Bernstein 1994, Corollary 1, equivalently Lagarias 1985: if the parity
vector of `x` is eventually periodic then `x` is rational. Elementary proof:
a purely periodic parity pattern of period `p` with `k` ones makes `T^p` act
on its cylinder as `y -> (3^k y + c)/2^p` with `c` an integer; `2^p - 3^k`
is odd, so the unique fixed point `y* = c/(2^p - 3^k)` lies in `Z_2`;
periodicity gives `T^(jp)(y) - y* = (3^(jk)/2^(jp))(y - y*)` in `Z_2` for
every `j`, so `|y - y*|_2 <= 2^(-jp)` for every `j` and `y = y*`; preimages
of a rational under finitely many maps `z -> 2z`, `z -> (2z-1)/3` are
rational.

The converse, `Q^{-1}(Q cap Z_2) = Q cap Z_2`, is Lagarias's Periodicity
Conjecture (1985; Bernstein and Lagarias 1996 p. 3) and is open; it implies
that no `3x+k` map with `k = +-1 mod 6` has a divergent integer trajectory
(Lagarias, Acta Arith. 56 (1990), Corollary 2.1b). So the usable direction
for P1 is the proved one, in contrapositive: an irrational 2-adic point has
a non-eventually-periodic parity vector.

The section-1 conditional fails for three independent reasons: the only
point whose parity vector is the column is `xi_30`, which carries no seed
information (section 3); periodicity would give a rational point, not a
positive integer, and the 3x+1 conjecture says nothing about rationals with
odd denominator (equivalently the `3x+k` maps, where nontrivial cycles are
known, e.g. for `3x+5`); and the direction it needs is the open one.

**5. The residue: one explicit sufficient arithmetic target.**

Let `c_0 c_1 c_2 ...` be the actual center column, `s = sum_t c_t 2^t` in
`Z_2` (written `B_30` in the originating discussion), and `d_0 < d_1 < ...`
the positions of its ones. Classically the column is eventually periodic if
and only if `s` is rational. Bernstein's non-iterative form of `Q^{-1}`
(1994, abstract and Theorem 1) gives the unique 2-adic point whose Collatz
parity vector is the column:

    xi_30 = Q^{-1}(s) = - sum_(j>=0) 2^(d_j) / 3^(j+1).

By Corollary 1, `s` rational implies `xi_30` rational. Therefore

    xi_30 irrational in Z_2  implies  P1 (the center column is not eventually periodic).

This uses no Collatz conjecture and no assumption about `H`. Two
distinctions stay explicit:

- `s` irrational is equivalent to P1, by the ordinary rationality criterion
  for 2-adic digit expansions. The Collatz map adds the different,
  sufficient target `xi_30` irrational. Necessity of that target is not
  proved: it would follow from the Periodicity Conjecture, which is open.
- `xi_30` is not established to equal `J` of any seed-derived point;
  section 3 shows `J(seed) = 1`.

This is the entire non-vacuous content of the Collatz reframing. It is a
restatement, not a result: the definition of `xi_30` consumes the center
sequence itself, so writing the series supplies no progress by itself. The
open obligation is to derive arithmetic structure of `xi_30` directly from
the local rule and the singleton origin, without passing through the
column, of a kind that supports an irrationality proof (the `3^(-j)`
weighting suggests Mahler-type methods; no evidence either way was
gathered). A seed-specific irrationality argument would require a
mathematical mechanism separate from the retired equidistribution route.
[AUDIT-adjacent-research-targets.md](AUDIT-adjacent-research-targets.md)
section 6 records the nearest known implications, from rational-base
normality to Mahler-type and generalized stopping problems, and that no
implication from a Rule 30 prize conclusion is established; nothing there
runs toward P1 either.

**6. Literature.**

No published connection between the 3x+1 problem and Rule 30 was found
(arXiv and OpenAlex searches for the pair, the Rule 30 prize page, and
Lagarias's annotated bibliography). Nearest items: Kopra, TCS 946 (2022),
places Rule 30 and fractional-multiplication automata in one rapidly left
expansive class, which also contains Rule 90 and never mentions Collatz
(already audited in AUDIT-adjacent-research-targets.md section 6); Kari, DLT
2012, relates a base-6 multiply-by-3 automaton to Collatz without Rule 30.
Conway (1972; Lagarias 1985 Theorem P) shows a generalized Collatz-type
function with an undecidable power-of-2 reachability problem, so the shared
feature of the two families is that each contains universal members. That
explains why closed forms are not expected; it is not a technique for either
instance.

1-Lipschitz maps RESULTS-anashin.md lines 53-66 show that producing such a
point is itself equivalent to settling P1; `H` and `T` are not 1-Lipschitz,
and for them no such independently described point is known.
